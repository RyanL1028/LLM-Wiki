**When you look at cells through a microscope, you will see smaller parts inside them. These parts all have important functions (jobs). Animal cells and plant cells contain some of the same parts. However, some parts are different.**

### What is inside an animal cell?

You can see some animal cells in Figure 1. They have an irregular shape. They contain four parts: a **nucleus**, a **cell membrane**, **cytoplasm**, and many **mitochondria** (singular: mitochondrion).

![Round cells packed tightly as viewed through a microscope. Each cell has a dark rounded nucleus inside it.](images/9781382063821_graphic_069.jpg)

### What is inside a plant cell?

Plant cells, like the one in Figure 3, have a more regular shape than animal cells. This allows them to fit together like bricks. They contain seven parts. Like animal cells, they contain a nucleus, a cell membrane, cytoplasm, and many mitochondria. They also contain three extra parts: a **cell wall**, a **vacuole**, and **chloroplasts**.

![A leaf cell that is rectangular in shape, containing numerous rounded structures around the inner edge of the cell.](images/9781382063821_graphic_071.jpg)

*Figure 3Leaf cells viewed under a microscope. Can you see the chloroplasts?*

# Physics

**Physics is the study of the physical world. In this unit, you will find out about [[Forces]] and where they come from, and how they explain [[Motion]] (the way objects move). You will learn about [[Light]] and [[Sound]] and ways we model them, and discover how we see and hear things.**

Physics affects you every day, even while you are asleep. It is how you interact with objects in the world, like your chair. It is how we design objects to make our lives easier, cleaner, and faster. It is how transport works, so we can get to school or to other countries. It is why music sounds the way it does, and how we can see inside the human body without surgery. It is how sports work, from football to skiing. Studying physics will help you understand how things work in the world and how you can make a difference.

Did you know that we have muscles in our eyes? The eye is a complex organ that works with the brain to interpret light. Light travels in waves just like other forms of [[Energy]]. Scientists use ideas about light to make sense of what we see. Our senses of hearing and seeing protect us from danger.

Forces act on objects to make them move, change direction, or stop. This is the study of motion. The relationship between the distance an object moves and the time it takes is its [[Speed]]. Motion graphs are used to show how objects move.

The amount of force per area is described as [[Pressure]]. This can occur in solids, liquids, and gases, and has many uses. Air pressure is used to inflate tyres and water pressure is used in hydraulics.

## Physics and the world

From microphones and lightbulbs to Wi-Fi and mobiles, technology depends on physics. We also use it in medical technology to look after and repair our bodies. If you learn to drive when you are older, understanding how cars speed up, slow down, and change direction can help keep you safe. Physics lets us send people to the Moon, robots to Mars, and spacecraft beyond the Solar System.

> **Big Question**
> **What is motion?**
> The speed that an object can travel at and the time it takes to stop are an area of science called motion. Scientists use motion graphs to illustrate the movement of objects.

> **Big Question**
> **Why is the sky blue?**
> Sunlight is a mixture of different colours. The colours get scattered (spread out) in different ways when the sunlight arrives in our atmosphere. The blue light gets scattered more than the other colours. This is why the sky looks blue most of the time.

> **Big Question**
> **What is the fastest speed limit?**
> Light travels at 186 000 miles per second. That is 300 000 kilometres per second. There is nothing faster than light!

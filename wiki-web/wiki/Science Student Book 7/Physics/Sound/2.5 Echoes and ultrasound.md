**Where is the quietest place in the world? Scientists have designed a room so quiet that you can hear your own heartbeat. The surfaces are designed to absorb (take in) sounds. There are no echoes.**

### What is an echo?

When sound reflects off a surface it produces an **echo**. Sound takes time to travel. There is a time delay (short wait) between making a sound and hearing an echo. Soft surfaces like curtains, or specially designed surfaces like those in Figure 1, can reduce echoes.

![A professional microphone set up in a recording studio. Acoustic foam panels for soundproofing are visible in the background.](images/9781382063821_graphic_305.jpg)

*Figure 1These walls are designed to produce no echoes.*

### Measuring distances

Imagine someone is standing a long distance from a building, as in Figure 2. They clap their hands and hear an echo two seconds later. The time delay depends on the distance.

You can use the time it takes to hear an echo to work out how far away objects are from the source of the sound.

### What are the uses of ultrasound?

Ultrasound is sound with a frequency above 20 000 Hz. An ultrasonic **transmitter** produces a beam, or line, of ultrasound. You can produce a narrow beam of ultrasound more easily than a narrow beam of sound waves.

When radiographers make images of unborn babies (see Figure 3), a beam of ultrasound travels through the body and reflects off the fetus. The ultrasonic **receiver** detects the echo. It uses the time it takes for the echo to return to build up an image of the fetus.

![Side-by-side comparison of an ultrasound image and a newborn baby. The ultrasound shows a detailed profile of the baby before birth, highlighting similarities to the actual appearance after birth.](images/9781382063821_graphic_307.jpg)

*Figure 3An ultrasound image (left) of a baby (right).*

> **C**
> Suggest whether ultrasound travels faster in bone or in tissue.

Physiotherapists can use ultrasound to reduce the pain and swelling of a damaged tendon. Doctors can use ultrasound to identify cancer. You can also use ultrasound to clean dirty objects.

### Where is ultrasound used to measure distance?

Figure 4 shows ultrasound being used on ships at sea. This is called **sonar**. A transmitter under the ship sends out a beam of ultrasound. This travels through the water and reflects off the seabed. A receiver detects the reflection. It uses the time taken to work out the depth of the water.

![A diagram showing how ships use ultrasound to measure ocean depth. The ship transmits an ultrasound beam through a transmitter towards the ocean floor. The sound wave reflects back as an echo, which is received by the ship through a receiver.](images/9781382063821_graphic_308.svg)

*Figure 4Ships use ultrasound to work out the depth of the ocean below them.*

> **Maths skills**
> A ship’s sonar detects an echo 1.6 s after it sends the pulse. The speed of sound in water is 1500 m/s.

Animals also use ultrasound. Bats and dolphins use ultrasound to find their food. They use sound and ultrasound to communicate with each other.

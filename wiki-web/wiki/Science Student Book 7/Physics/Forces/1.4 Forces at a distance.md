> **Key idea**
> Non-contact [[Forces]] act from a distance in a field. Magnetic forces, electrostatic forces, and gravity are all non-contact forces.

> **Key words**
> magnetic force, electrostatic force, field, weight, mass, kilogram (kg), [[Gravitational Field Strength]]

**If you let go of your pen and it moved up towards the ceiling, you would be very surprised. We are so familiar with the force of gravity that sometimes we do not even think of it as a force.**

### Which forces act ‘at a distance’?

A [[Gravitational Force]] acts on your pen when you hold it up and let it go. Gravity is a non-contact force, and it is always attractive. There are other types of non-contact force.

Magnets exert a **magnetic force** on magnetic materials or other magnets without touching them. If you rub a balloon, you can pick up bits of paper with it or make your hair stand on end. You can see this happening in Figure 1. This is an electric or **electrostatic force**. Magnetic and electrostatic forces are non-contact forces that can be attractive or repulsive.

### What is a field?

In physics, a **field** is a special region in which something experiences a force. In a magnetic field, magnetic things experience a force. In a gravitational field, things with mass experience a force.

Gravitational, magnetic, and electrostatic fields have something in common. As you get further away from the mass, magnet, or charge, the field gets weaker.

Contact forces only act when objects are touching each other. Non-contact forces act at any distance, if the objects are touching or if they are *not* touching.

### What do I weigh?

You can use a newton meter to find the **weight** of an apple. Earth pulls the apple down, towards the ground. Measuring the weight of the apple means measuring the force of Earth on it.

### What is the difference between weight and mass?

Weight is a force, so it is measured in newtons (N). **Mass** is the amount of ‘stuff’ something is made up of. It is a measure of how hard it is to get something to move. Mass is measured in **kilograms (kg)**. Figure 2 shows one way of measuring weight.

### What would happen to my weight in space?

Imagine firing off from Earth in a spacecraft. As you move away from Earth, its gravitational field gets weaker. If you stood on scales (a balance) in the spacecraft, the reading would be less than it would be on Earth. Eventually the scale would read zero, but the amount of ‘you’ would not change — your mass stays the same. It is the force of Earth on you, your weight, that gets smaller.

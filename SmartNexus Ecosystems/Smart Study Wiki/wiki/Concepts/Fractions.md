A **fraction** has two parts: a **numerator** (top) and a **denominator** (bottom). The denominator tells you how many equal parts the whole is split into. The numerator tells you how many of those parts you have.

### Equivalent Fractions

Two fractions are **equivalent** if they represent the same value. You can find an equivalent fraction by multiplying or dividing both the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number.

<div class="math-box">
<strong>Example:</strong> 3/4 = 6/8 = 9/12 = 12/16 — all are equivalent because each is obtained by multiplying top and bottom by the same number.
</div>

### Simplifying Fractions

To **simplify** a fraction, divide both numerator and denominator by their highest common factor. A fraction is in its simplest form when the numerator and denominator have no common factors other than 1.

<div class="math-box">
<strong>Example:</strong> Simplify 12/18. The HCF of 12 and 18 is 6, so 12 ÷ 6 = 2 and 18 ÷ 6 = 3. Answer: 2/3.
</div>

### Mixed Numbers and Improper Fractions

An **improper fraction** has a numerator larger than its denominator (e.g. 11/4). A **mixed number** combines a whole number with a proper fraction (e.g. 2 3/4).

To convert an improper fraction to a mixed number: divide the numerator by the denominator — the quotient is the whole number, the remainder goes over the original denominator.

To convert a mixed number to an improper fraction: multiply the whole number by the denominator, add the numerator, and place over the original denominator.

### Comparing and Ordering Fractions

To compare fractions with different denominators, find a common denominator (usually the LCM) and convert each fraction.

<div class="math-box">
<strong>Example:</strong> Compare 2/3 and 3/5. LCM of 3 and 5 is 15. 2/3 = 10/15, 3/5 = 9/15, so 2/3 > 3/5.
</div>

### Adding and Subtracting Fractions

**Same denominator:** Add or subtract the numerators, keep the denominator the same.

**Different denominators:** Find a common denominator first, convert both fractions, then add or subtract.

<ol class="step-list">
<li>Find the LCM of the denominators</li>
<li>Convert each fraction to an equivalent fraction with the LCM as denominator</li>
<li>Add or subtract the numerators</li>
<li>Simplify the result and convert to a mixed number if needed</li>
</ol>

### Multiplying Fractions

Multiply the numerators together and the denominators together. Simplify the result.

<div class="math-box">
<strong>Example:</strong> 3/4 × 2/5 = (3 × 2)/(4 × 5) = 6/20 = 3/10.
</div>

To multiply a fraction by an integer: write the integer as a fraction over 1, then multiply as above. For mixed numbers, convert to improper fractions first.

### Dividing Fractions

To divide by a fraction, **multiply by its reciprocal** (flip the second fraction and multiply).

<div class="math-box">
<strong>Example:</strong> 2/3 ÷ 5 = 2/3 × 1/5 = 2/15.
</div>

### Fractions of an Amount

To find a fraction of a quantity, divide by the denominator and multiply by the numerator.

<div class="math-box">
<strong>Example:</strong> Find 3/8 of 64. 64 ÷ 8 = 8, 8 × 3 = 24. Answer: 24.
</div>

### Fractional Increase and Decrease

To increase an amount by a fraction: find the fraction of the amount and add it to the original. To decrease: find the fraction and subtract.

# Characteristics and Classification of Living Organisms

**Summary**: All living organisms share seven key characteristics (MRS GREN). Organisms are classified using the binomial naming system into a hierarchy of groups: Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species. The five kingdoms are Animals, Plants, Fungi, Prokaryotes, and Protoctists. Viruses are not considered living organisms.

## 1. Characteristics of Living Organisms — MRS GREN

All living organisms share seven key characteristics. The acronym **MRS GREN** helps to remember them:

| Letter | Characteristic | Meaning |
|--------|---------------|---------|
| **M** | **Movement** | An action by an organism or part of an organism causing a change of position or place |
| **R** | **Respiration** | The chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules to release energy for metabolism |
| **S** | **Sensitivity** | The ability to detect and respond to changes in the internal or external environment (stimuli) |
| **G** | **Growth** | A permanent increase in size and dry mass by an increase in cell number or cell size |
| **R** | **Reproduction** | The processes that make more of the same kind of organism |
| **E** | **Excretion** | The removal of the waste products of metabolism (e.g. carbon dioxide, urea) and substances in excess of requirements |
| **N** | **Nutrition** | The taking in of materials for energy, growth, and development |

**Important distinction — Excretion vs Egestion:**
- **Excretion** = removal of metabolic waste (CO2 from respiration, urea from breakdown of amino acids)
- **Egestion** = removal of undigested food (faeces) — this is NOT a characteristic of life

## 2. Classification Hierarchy

Organisms are grouped into a hierarchical system of classification:

```
Kingdom -> Phylum -> Class -> Order -> Family -> Genus -> Species
```

A useful mnemonic: **K**ing **P**hilip **C**ame **O**ver **F**or **G**ood **S**oup

- **Species**: A group of organisms that can reproduce to produce fertile offspring
- As you go **down** the hierarchy, organisms become **more similar**
- As you go **up** the hierarchy, groups become **larger and more diverse**

## 3. The Binomial Naming System

Developed by Carl Linnaeus. Every species is given a two-part scientific name:

- **Genus** (capitalised first letter) + **species** (all lowercase)
- Written in *italics* when typed, or underlined when handwritten
- Same worldwide — avoids confusion from different common names in different languages

**Examples:**
- *Homo sapiens* — Modern humans
- *Panthera leo* — Lion
- *Escherichia coli* — A bacterium found in the gut
- *Canis lupus* — Grey wolf

## 4. The Five Kingdoms

| Kingdom | Key Features | Examples |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| **Animals** | Multicellular, no cell wall, no chloroplasts, heterotrophic (feed on other organisms), usually able to move | Humans, insects, fish, birds |
| **Plants** | Multicellular, have cell walls (cellulose), contain chloroplasts, autotrophic (photosynthesis), store carbohydrates as starch | Flowering plants, ferns, mosses |
| **Fungi** | Usually multicellular (except yeast — unicellular), cell walls made of chitin, no chloroplasts, saprophytic or parasitic nutrition (feed by secreting enzymes onto food and absorbing the digested products), store carbohydrates as glycogen, body usually organised into a mycelium made of thread-like hyphae | Mushrooms, mould, yeast |
| **Prokaryotes** (Bacteria) | Unicellular, no true nucleus (DNA is a single circular chromosome in the cytoplasm), no membrane-bound organelles, have a cell wall (not made of cellulose), some can photosynthesise, reproduce by binary fission | *E. coli*, *Salmonella*, *Lactobacillus* |
| **Protoctists** | Mostly unicellular (some multicellular), have a true nucleus (eukaryotic), may have chloroplasts (e.g. algae) or not (e.g. amoeba), some are pathogenic | *Amoeba*, *Paramecium*, *Chlorella*, *Plasmodium* (causes malaria) |

## 5. Vertebrate Classes

All vertebrates have a **backbone** (vertebral column). There are five main classes:

| Class | Body Covering | Breathing | Reproduction | Thermoregulation | Examples |
|-------|--------------|-----------|--------------|------------------|----------|
| **Mammals** | Hair/fur | Lungs | Internal fertilisation, live birth, mammary glands produce milk for young | Endothermic (warm-blooded) | Humans, whales, bats |
| **Birds** | Feathers | Lungs | Internal fertilisation, lay hard-shelled eggs | Endothermic (warm-blooded) | Eagle, penguin, robin |
| **Reptiles** | Dry, scaly skin | Lungs | Internal fertilisation, lay soft-shelled eggs on land | Ectothermic (cold-blooded) | Snake, lizard, crocodile |
| **Amphibians** | Moist, smooth skin (no scales) | Gills (larvae), lungs and skin (adults) | External fertilisation, lay soft jelly-coated eggs in water | Ectothermic (cold-blooded) | Frog, toad, salamander |
| **Fish** | Scales (with mucus coating) | Gills | External fertilisation, lay eggs in water | Ectothermic (cold-blooded) | Salmon, shark, goldfish |

**Key distinction:**
- **Endothermic** (warm-blooded): Body temperature is maintained internally, independent of the environment
- **Ectothermic** (cold-blooded): Body temperature varies with the environment — rely on external heat sources

## 6. Arthropods

Arthropods are invertebrates with **jointed legs** and an **exoskeleton** (external skeleton made of chitin).

| Group | Body Segments | Legs | Other Features | Examples |
|-------|--------------|------|---------------|----------|
| **Insects** | 3 parts (head, thorax, abdomen) | 3 pairs (6 legs) | Usually 2 pairs of wings, 1 pair of antennae | Butterfly, ant, bee, beetle |
| **Arachnids** | 2 parts (cephalothorax, abdomen) | 4 pairs (8 legs) | No antennae, simple eyes | Spider, scorpion, tick |
| **Crustaceans** | 2 parts (cephalothorax, abdomen) | 5+ pairs (10+ legs) | 2 pairs of antennae, mostly aquatic | Crab, lobster, woodlouse |
| **Myriapods** | Many similar segments | Many pairs of legs (1 or 2 pairs per segment) | 1 pair of antennae | Centipede, millipede |

## 7. Plant Classification — Ferns and Flowering Plants

**Ferns:**
- Have true roots, stems, and leaves (fronds)
- Leaves have **spores** on the underside (reproduce by spores, not seeds)
- Have vascular tissue (xylem and phloem)

**Flowering Plants (Angiosperms):** Divided into monocotyledons (monocots) and dicotyledons (dicots).

| Feature | Monocotyledons | Dicotyledons |
|---------|---------------|--------------|
| Number of cotyledons (seed leaves) | 1 | 2 |
| Leaf venation | Parallel veins | Branched/reticulate (net-like) veins |
| Vascular bundles in stem | Scattered | Arranged in a ring |
| Flower parts (petals etc.) | In multiples of 3 | In multiples of 4 or 5 |
| Root system | Fibrous roots | Taproot with lateral roots |
| Examples | Grasses, wheat, maize, lilies | Sunflowers, beans, roses, oak trees |

## 8. Viruses — Not Living Organisms

Viruses are **not classified as living** because:
- They have **no cellular structure** (no nucleus, no cytoplasm, no cell membrane, no organelles)
- They **cannot reproduce on their own** — they must infect a host cell and use the host's machinery to replicate
- They do **not carry out any of the MRS GREN life processes** independently
- They do **not respire, grow, or excrete**

**Structure of a virus:**
- A protein coat (**capsid**) surrounding genetic material (DNA or RNA)
- Some have an additional lipid envelope (e.g. influenza virus, HIV)

**Examples:**
- Tobacco mosaic virus (TMV) — infects plants, causes discolouration of leaves
- Influenza virus — causes flu in humans
- HIV — attacks the immune system

## Common Misconceptions

| Misconception | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Excretion is the same as egestion" | Excretion removes metabolic waste (CO2, urea); egestion removes undigested food (faeces) |
| "All bacteria are harmful" | Many bacteria are useful — decomposers, nitrogen-fixing bacteria, gut flora, used in food production (yogurt, cheese) |
| "Fungi are plants" | Fungi belong to their own kingdom — they have cell walls of chitin (not cellulose) and cannot photosynthesise |
| "All protoctists are unicellular" | Some protoctists (e.g. certain algae, seaweeds) are multicellular |
| "Viruses are the smallest living organism" | Viruses are NOT living organisms — they have no cellular structure and cannot carry out life processes independently |
| "Fish are endothermic" | Fish are ectothermic — their body temperature changes with the surrounding water temperature |
| "All arachnids are spiders" | Arachnids also include scorpions, ticks, and mites — all have 8 legs and 2 body parts |

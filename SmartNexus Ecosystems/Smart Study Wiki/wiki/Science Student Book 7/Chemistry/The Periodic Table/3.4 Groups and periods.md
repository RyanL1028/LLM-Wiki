**Palladium is a metal. What can you predict about its properties?**

Figure 1 shows that palladium is shiny. You might have predicted that it is a good conductor of thermal [[Energy]] (heat) and of electricity. If you find palladium in the Periodic Table, you can make even better predictions.

![A bar of palladium with an image of a woman carved on it. It looks shiny and hard.](images/9781382063821_graphic_247.jpg)

*Figure 1Palladium is a metal element. It is used in catalytic converters in cars, in tools used for surgery in hospitals, and in some musical instruments.*

### What are groups?

In the Periodic Table, the columns are called **groups**. Figure 2 shows some groups of the Periodic Table. For example, the elements beryllium (Be) and magnesium (Mg) are in Group 2.

![Part of the Periodic Table with a focus on group numbers.](images/9781382063821_graphic_248.svg)

*Figure 2Some groups of the Periodic Table.  Full text alternative to the content*

The elements in a group have similar physical and chemical properties. Going down a group, there are patterns in properties.

Sophie studies the data. She makes this prediction:

*For the groups with iron and cobalt, melting point increases from top to bottom. The group with nickel is likely to show the same pattern. So I predict that the melting point of palladium is between 1453 °C and 1769 °C.*

A data book gives the melting point of palladium as 1550 °C. Sophie’s prediction is correct.

# Chemistry

**Chemistry is the study of matter, the stuff that everything is made of. In this unit, you will learn what matter is made of and how this explains its properties. You will explore how matter changes when heated and cooled, and why these changes happen.**

Chemistry is everywhere. It happens in nature and in the laboratory. Nothing and nowhere is free of chemicals.

Chemistry is everything you see, smell, and touch. It provides clean water and warm clothes. It makes materials of beauty, strength, and flexibility. It makes materials with properties perfect for their purpose, like glass for phones, nylon for clothes, wood for furniture, and explosives for fireworks.

Chemistry cooks and breaks down food. It makes muscle, bone, and hair. Its smells can make you feel hungry or warn you of danger. Chemistry keeps you alive.

Chemistry is united by one big idea: tiny particles – and the way in which they are arranged, rearrange, move, and separate – explain the properties of everything and control how to make everything else.

## Chemistry and the world

Understanding chemistry helps us use [[Products]] safely, take the correct dose of a medicine, and cook the food we eat.

Chemistry helps us make decisions that are good for the environment, and work out what is true and what is not. Will you buy clothes made of cotton or nylon, or only recycled clothes?

> **Big Question**
> **What gives materials their properties?**
> Scientists use particle models to show how [[Atoms]] are arranged in different materials. This determines their properties (what materials look like, feel like, and how they behave). This is why we choose different materials for different uses in our daily lives.

> **Big Question**
> **What is the [[Periodic Table]]?**
> Scientists have identified over 100 [[Elements]]. They are organized in groups and periods in the Periodic Table. Their position is determined by trends in their behaviour.

> **Big Question**
> **How are [[Acids]] useful?**
> The acid in our stomach breaks down the food we eat and kills bad bacteria so we do not get sick. We also use acids in cleaning products and in cars. Scientists use indicators to identify acids. They use the pH scale to find out how strong an acid is and how to use it safely.

### What are the states of matter?

Water can exist in three states: as a solid, a liquid, or a gas. These are the states of matter. In each of the three states, the water particles are identical (the same). But the properties of ice, liquid water, and steam are different. These pages explain why.

### How can particles explain properties?

The particles of a substance do not change. Water particles are the same in all three states. But the arrangement and movement of the particles - and how strongly they hold together - are different in each state.

Figure 1 shows particles of a substance in the solid state. When a substance is in the solid state, its particles touch their neighbours. This explains why you cannot compress it (press it into a smaller shape). In the solid state, particles are arranged in a pattern. This explains why crystals have straight edges and smooth faces.

![Particles in a solid substance are tightly arranged in a regular pattern.](images/9781382063821_graphic_173.svg)

*Figure 1The arrangement of particles in a solid substance.*

When a substance is in the solid state, its particles do not move around. Instead, they vibrate (shake) on the spot. This explains why solids have a fixed shape and why they cannot flow.

Figure 2 shows particles of a substance in the liquid state. When a substance is in the liquid state, its particles touch their neighbours. This explains why you cannot compress it. The particles move in different directions from place to place, moving over each other. This explains why liquids flow and why they have no fixed shape.

![Particles in a liquid substance touch each other but are loosely arranged in an irregular pattern.](images/9781382063821_graphic_174.svg)

*Figure 2The arrangement of particles in a liquid substance.*

Figure 3 shows particles of a substance in the gas state. When a substance is in the gas state, its particles spread out until they are far apart. This explains why it is easy to compress a gas. The particles move quickly and in different directions, everywhere in the container. This explains why gases flow.

![Particles in a gas substance are far apart.](images/9781382063821_graphic_175.svg)

*Figure 3The arrangement of particles in a gas substance.*

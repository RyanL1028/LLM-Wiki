# 4 Motion and pressure

![](images/9781382063821_graphic_369.jpg)
> **In this chapter, you will:**
> - learn how to interpret a [[Motion]] graph, which is a graph of distance against time
> - calculate [[Speed]] from data or from a motion graph
> - find out how the [[Pressure]] of a gas depends on temperature or volume, and why the atmosphere exerts (applies) a pressure that changes with height
> - compare pressure in gases and pressure in liquids
> - calculate the pressure exerted on the surface of a solid
> - learn how [[Forces]] can make objects turn around a pivot, and why some objects are more balanced than others.

> **Think back**
> 1. Describe a situation where the forces on a moving object are balanced, and another where they are unbalanced.
> 2. Compare the way in which particles are arranged and move in the liquid state and the gas state.
> 3. Calculate the area of a square with a side of 10 cm, and the area of a rectangle of sides 10 cm and 20 cm.

> **Key ideas**
> Objects are stationary (still) or move at a steady speed unless a [[Resultant Force]] acts. A resultant force can make an object speed up, slow down, or change direction.
> The particles in a substance are arranged differently in the solid, liquid, and gas states. The particles in a gas move faster if the temperature of the gas increases.
> The area of a regular shape, like a square or a rectangle, is calculated by multiplying its height by its width. The unit of area is either cm² or m².

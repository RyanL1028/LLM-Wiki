### Seeing sound

You can attach a **microphone** to an **oscilloscope**, like the one in Figure 1, to see what the sound of your voice looks like. The microphone produces an electrical signal that represents the sound wave. The wave that appears on the screen is transverse. However, the sound waves you make when you talk are longitudinal.

### What affects the loudness of a sound?

If a drummer hits a drum harder, the sound is louder.

You can bang a drum harder or hit a xylophone harder with a mallet to produce a louder sound. A loud sound has a larger amplitude than a soft sound. It also transfers more [[Energy]]. To make a louder sound, you need to make the amplitude bigger.

### How do you measure loudness?

Sounds made by volcanic eruptions are so loud that they can be heard thousands of miles away. Sound intensity determines **loudness** and is measured in **decibels** (dB). The decibel scale shown in Table 1 is not like a ruler. Each increase of 10 dB increases the intensity of sound by ten times.

### What affects the pitch of a sound?

Some singers can produce higher-pitched notes than others. The **pitch** of a note depends on the frequency of the sound wave. High-pitched sounds have a high frequency and low-pitched sounds have a low frequency. Frequency is measured in **hertz** (Hz) or **kilohertz** (kHz), and 1000 Hz = 1 kHz.

To make a higher-pitched sound, you need to make something vibrate faster. Faster vibration means there are more waves per second. You can see the difference in frequency between a high-pitched and a low-pitched sound in Figure 3.

![Two people outdoors, with one blowing into a whistle. There are two wave diagrams labelled as low and high.](images/9781382063821_graphic_386.svg)

*Figure 3A high sound has a higher frequency than a low sound. A whistle produces a sound with a high frequency.  Full text alternative to the content*

You can have a loud, high-pitched sound or a loud, low-pitched sound. Changing the frequency of a wave, such as sound, does not affect its amplitude.

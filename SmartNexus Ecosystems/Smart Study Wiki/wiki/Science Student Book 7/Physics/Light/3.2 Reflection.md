### Why might you see an image in the mirror?

When you look in the mirror, you might see someone who looks just like you behind the mirror. There is an **image** in the mirror.

Light travels in straight lines. The brain uses this fact to work out where the light appears to be coming from. This is where you see the image. It is a **virtual** image because light has not travelled from it to the eye. You can see how this works in Figure 2.

![A diagram showing how a person sees an image in a mirror. Light rays from an object reflect off the mirror and travel to the person's eyes, creating a virtual image of the object behind the mirror.](images/9781382063821_graphic_316.svg)

*Figure 2You can see an image in a mirror.*

When something is reflected in a flat, or **plane**, mirror the image is:
- the same shape and size as the real object
- as far behind the mirror as the real object is in front of the mirror.

Left and right appear swapped (changed positions), but the mirror is actually swapping back and front.

> If you stand 1 m in front of a mirror, looking directly at it, how far are you from your image?

### What is the law of reflection?

You know that you need light to be reflected off an object for you to see it. Light reflects off a mirror in the same way that a wave reflects off a barrier, or a sound wave reflects off a building.

There is an imaginary line at an angle of 90° to the mirror. This is called the **normal**. You measure angles from the normal to the rays of light. You can see how to do this in Figure 3.

![A ray diagram showing the reflection of light on a mirror.](images/9781382063821_graphic_317.svg)

*Figure 3Light is reflected at equal angles.  Full text alternative to the content*

The angle between the incident ray (the ray coming in) and the normal is the **angle of incidence**. The angle between the normal and the reflected ray (the ray coming out) is the **angle of reflection**.

When light is reflected off a mirror, the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection. This is the **law of reflection**.

> **B**
> If the angle of incidence is 30o, what is the angle of reflection?

### How does light reflect off rough surfaces?

Every surface reflects at least some light. Figure 4 shows how light is reflected off two different surfaces. You can only see your image in surfaces that reflect light in a regular way.

To form an image, the rays from each part of the object have to reflect off a surface in the same way (see Figure 4a). If two rays that are parallel are reflected off a surface at different angles, you won’t see an image (see Figure 4b).

![Two illustrations labelled a and b depicting reflection that occurs on a smooth and rough surface.](images/9781382063821_graphic_318.svg)

*Figure 4You can only see an image in a smooth surface.  Full text alternative to the content*

**You may sometimes notice your stomach making noises. This is a sign that you need to eat. You know that food contains nutrients. But how does your body get nutrients out of food?**

### What is the digestive system?

The **digestive system** is a group of organs that work together to break down food. The nutrients in most of the food you eat are large molecules, like lipids and proteins. During **digestion**, these large molecules are broken down into small nutrient molecules. These nutrients can then pass into the blood where they are used by the body. Figure 1 shows this process.

### Moving through the digestive system

Fibre in your food is not digested. However, fibre adds bulk (roughage) to the food. Muscles push against this, forcing food along the gut. Figure 3 shows this process. Eating lots of foods containing fibre, such as vegetables and wholemeal bread, helps prevent constipation.

![A sequence of three diagrams showing the movement of food through the gut. The walls of the gut are shown contracting and relaxing in different sections to move the food forward.](images/9781382063821_graphic_419.svg)

*Figure 3Muscles in the wall of the gut push food along – a bit like pressing down on a tube of toothpaste.*

### Passing into the blood

The small nutrient molecules produced during digestion pass into the blood through the wall of the small intestine. They then move around the body.

The small intestine needs to absorb (take in) the nutrients quickly, before the undigested food passes out of the body. The small intestine is adapted to (designed for) this function. The wall of the small intestine is thin. It is also covered with tiny structures called **villi** (see Figure 4). Villi stick out of the wall and give it a big surface area. They also contain blood capillaries to carry away the absorbed food molecules.

![Villi in the small intestine as viewed through a microscope, showing numerous closely packed, finger-like tubular projections lining the intestinal wall.](images/9781382063821_graphic_142.jpg)

*Figure 4Villi in the small intestine increase the surface area. This allows more nutrients to be absorbed.*

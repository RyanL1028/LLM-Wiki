**Even when you are sitting still, your ribcage is moving. This allows your lungs to fill with oxygen and is essential for you to stay alive.**

### How do you breathe?

When you breathe, muscles in your chest get shorter. They contract. Figure 1 shows a bell-jar model. This model can show you what is happening inside your lungs when you breathe in and out. The jar represents your chest. The balloons represent your lungs. The rubber sheet represents a muscle called the **diaphragm**.

### How can we measure lung volume?

Figure 4 shows how you can measure your **lung volume** using a plastic bottle. When you breathe out into the plastic tube, air from your lungs takes the place of the water in the bottle. If you breathe out as much as you can, the volume of water pushed out of the bottle is equal to how much air your lungs can hold.

![A tank of water containing an inverted plastic jug and a plastic tube with one end connected to the mouth of the jug and the other end out of the tank. The jug is full of water along with some air bubbles.](images/9781382063821_graphic_098.svg)

*Figure 4You can measure your lung volume by breathing into a bottle.*

Regular exercise can help increase your lung volume. A large lung volume means that more oxygen can enter your body. Smoking, diseases such as **asthma**, and old age can reduce lung volume.

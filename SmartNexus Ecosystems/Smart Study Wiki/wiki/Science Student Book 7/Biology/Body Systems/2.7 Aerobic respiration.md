**Glucose is a type of sugar. It provides all the energy that cells need. Plants produce glucose by [[Photosynthesis]]. Humans get glucose from carbohydrate foods like rice, bread, and pasta.**

### How do cells transfer energy?

Your body needs energy for everything it does. You need energy to move, to grow, and to keep warm. Energy is required all the time (even when you are asleep!) to keep your body working.

You get your energy from organic [[Molecules]] in the food you eat.

To transfer the energy stored in food, glucose reacts with oxygen in a chemical reaction called **aerobic respiration**. This reaction transfers energy to your cells. The waste products carbon dioxide and water are also formed.

### Where does respiration happen?

Respiration happens inside tiny structures inside your cells called **mitochondria**. Figure 1 shows a mitochondrion (singular). All cells contain mitochondria. However, different cells contain different amounts. Muscle cells carry out lots of respiration, so they contain lots of mitochondria.

![A mitochondrion as viewed through a microscope. It is an oval-shaped structure with a double membrane. The outer membrane is smooth, while the inner membrane is folded into long tubes.](images/9781382063821_graphic_160.jpg)

### How do glucose and oxygen get into cells?

Glucose and oxygen are carried in the blood.
- During digestion, carbohydrates are broken down, releasing glucose molecules. The molecules are absorbed into the blood through the wall of the small intestine.
- Glucose dissolves in the liquid part of your blood called plasma. It then diffuses into the cells that need it for respiration.
- When you breathe in, oxygen fills the alveoli in your lungs. The oxygen then diffuses into your blood.
- Oxygen joins to haemoglobin (a red protein) in your red blood cells. When it reaches a cell that needs oxygen, the oxygen diffuses into the cell.

### How does carbon dioxide leave the body?

If carbon dioxide remained in your body, it would build up to a dangerous level. You remove carbon dioxide when you exhale (breathe out).

Carbon dioxide produced during respiration diffuses out of your cells and into the blood plasma. The blood carries it to the lungs, where it diffuses into the alveoli, and is then exhaled.

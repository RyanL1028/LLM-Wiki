## Summary

This source introduces the difference between [[Atoms]] and [[Elements]], explains how the [[Periodic Table]] organises elements, and teaches the rules for writing [[Chemical Symbols]]. It emphasizes that bulk material properties emerge from large groups of atoms rather than single atoms.

## Core Ideas

- An element is made of one type of atom and cannot be chemically broken down further in this context.
- An atom is the smallest part of an element that can exist.
- Elements are represented by one- or two-letter symbols with fixed capitalization rules.
- Around 100 elements occur naturally, with more synthesized.

## Teaching and Assessment Focus

- Distinguish atoms vs elements clearly.
- Recall and apply chemical symbols correctly.
- Use periodic table groupings to reason about similar properties.
- Connect microscopic particle ideas to macroscopic properties (for example melting and density differences).

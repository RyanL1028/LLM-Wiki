# Carbon Dioxide (CO2)

**Summary**: Carbon dioxide (CO₂) is a linear covalent molecule containing **two C=O double bonds** — carbon reaches its octet through four shared pairs in total; it turns limewater milky, is produced by complete combustion and respiration, and is a major greenhouse gas.
**Tags**: #igcse #chemistry
**Created**: 2026-07-18

---

Carbon dioxide (CO₂) is a [[Compound]] of carbon and oxygen and the syllabus example of a [[Molecule]] containing **two double covalent bonds**. Carbon has four [[Valence Electron|valence electrons]] and needs four more for an octet, while each oxygen has six and needs two more; by [[Covalent Bonding|sharing electrons]], carbon forms a **double bond with each of the two oxygen atoms**, so the carbon atom is surrounded by **4 shared pairs in total** and every atom achieves a full outer [[Electron Shell]]. The molecule is **linear** (O=C=O) and, because only weak forces act between molecules, CO₂ is a gas at room temperature. It is produced by the **complete combustion** of carbon-containing fuels, by **respiration**, by the thermal decomposition of [[Carbonates]] and by the reaction of acids with carbonates, and it is identified because it turns **limewater milky** ([[Chemical Tests]]). Although it makes up only a small fraction of the [[Composition of Air]], carbon dioxide is a major **greenhouse gas**, and its rising concentration from burning fossil fuels drives global warming (see [[Climate Change and Greenhouse Gases]]).

## Bonding: Dot-and-Cross Description

In a [[Dot-and-Cross Diagram]] of CO₂, carbon's electrons are drawn as dots and the oxygens' as crosses:

- Carbon shares **2 of its electrons with each oxygen**, and each oxygen shares 2 electrons back, so **each C=O bond is a double bond containing 2 shared pairs** (two dots and two crosses).
- With two double bonds, carbon is surrounded by **4 shared pairs (8 electrons)** — a complete octet.
- Each oxygen counts 8 outer electrons: 4 in its double bond + **2 lone pairs** of its own.
- The two double bonds repel each other equally, so the molecule is **linear**: O=C=O, with a bond angle of 180°.

Displayed formula: **O=C=O**.

## Key Data

| Property | Carbon dioxide |
|---|---|
| Formula | CO₂ |
| Bonding | 2 × C=O double bonds (4 shared pairs at C); 2 lone pairs on each O |
| Shape / notes | Linear molecule (O=C=O); simple molecular structure — compare diamond/graphite in [[Giant Structures]] |
| State at r.t.p. | Colourless gas (solid CO₂, "dry ice", sublimes at −78 °C) |

## Physical Properties

- Simple molecular gas: low melting/boiling point because only weak [[Intermolecular Force|intermolecular forces]] are overcome — the strong C=O bonds are not broken on changing state.
- Denser than air — it can be "poured" and collects downwards, which is why it is used in fire extinguishers.
- Slightly soluble in water, forming weakly acidic carbonic acid (rain is naturally slightly acidic — see [[Acids and Bases]]).
- Does not conduct electricity — no [[Ion|ions]] or [[Delocalised Electron|delocalised electrons]].

## Test for Carbon Dioxide

Bubble the gas through **limewater** (calcium hydroxide solution). CO₂ turns it **milky/cloudy** as insoluble calcium carbonate forms:

Ca(OH)₂(aq) + CO₂(g) → CaCO₃(s) + H₂O(l)

With excess CO₂ the milkiness slowly clears as soluble calcium hydrogencarbonate forms. See [[Chemical Tests]] and [[Carbonates]].

## Sources and Roles

- **Complete combustion** of carbon-containing fuels: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O (see [[Fuels and Petroleum]]).
- **Respiration** in living cells releases CO₂ as a waste product.
- **Thermal decomposition** of carbonates, e.g. CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂, and **acid + carbonate** reactions (see [[Carbonates]] and [[Making Salts]]).
- **Greenhouse gas** — CO₂ absorbs infrared radiation, trapping heat in the atmosphere; increasing levels cause global warming and climate change (see [[Climate Change and Greenhouse Gases]] and [[Air Pollution]]).
- Used in fizzy drinks, fire extinguishers and as dry ice for cooling.

## Common Misconceptions

| Misconception | Reality |
|---|---|
| CO₂ contains single C–O bonds. | Each C–O link is a **double bond (2 shared pairs)**; carbon needs both double bonds to complete its octet of 4 shared pairs. |
| The CO₂ molecule is bent like water. | CO₂ is **linear** (O=C=O, 180°) because the two double bonds repel each other equally and carbon has no lone pairs. |
| Carbon dioxide and carbon monoxide are the same pollutant. | CO₂ is a **greenhouse gas** from complete combustion; **CO** is a toxic gas from *incomplete* combustion (see [[Air Pollution]]). |
| CO₂ turning limewater milky is a physical change. | It is a **chemical reaction** — insoluble calcium carbonate is precipitated. |

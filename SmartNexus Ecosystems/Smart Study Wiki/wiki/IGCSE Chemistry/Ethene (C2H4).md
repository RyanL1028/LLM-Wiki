# Ethene (C2H4)

**Summary**: Ethene (C₂H₄) is the simplest alkene — its two carbon atoms share **two** pairs of electrons in a carbon–carbon double covalent bond, and each carbon also forms two C–H bonds; as an unsaturated hydrocarbon it rapidly decolourises bromine water.
**Tags**: #igcse #chemistry
**Created**: 2026-07-18

---

Ethene (C₂H₄) is the first member of the [[Alkenes]] homologous series and the standard IGCSE example of a **double covalent bond** between two carbon atoms. Each carbon atom has four [[Valence Electron|valence electrons]] and needs four more to complete its octet; in ethene, the two carbons share **two pairs** of electrons with each other (the C=C double bond) and each carbon shares one further pair with each of two hydrogen atoms. This [[Covalent Bonding|sharing of electrons]] gives every carbon a full outer [[Electron Shell]] of 8 and every hydrogen the helium duplet, so the [[Molecule]] is stable without forming [[Ion|ions]]. Because the C=C double bond can "open up" to let more atoms add on, ethene is described as **unsaturated**, which makes it far more reactive than the single-bonded [[Alkanes]]. This reactivity is exploited industrially: thousands of ethene molecules join together by addition polymerisation to form poly(ethene), the most common of the [[Polymers]], and ethene also adds water (steam) to manufacture ethanol (see [[Alcohols]]).

## Bonding: Dot-and-Cross Description

In a [[Dot-and-Cross Diagram]] of C₂H₄, one carbon's electrons are drawn as dots and the other's as crosses (hydrogens' electrons in a third style or as crosses):

- The **C=C double bond** consists of **2 shared pairs** — four electrons (two dots and two crosses) drawn together in the region between the two carbon atoms.
- Each carbon also forms **two C–H single bonds** (one shared pair each), giving **four C–H bonds** in the molecule overall.
- Each carbon therefore counts 8 outer electrons: 4 from the double bond + 2 × 2 from its C–H bonds — a full octet.
- Each hydrogen counts 2 electrons — the helium duplet.
- There are **no lone pairs** in ethene; all 12 valence electrons are in bonding pairs.

Displayed formula: **H₂C=CH₂**. The molecule is flat (planar), with all six atoms in the same plane.

## Key Data

| Property | Ethene |
|---|---|
| Formula | C₂H₄ |
| Bonding | 1 × C=C double bond (2 shared pairs) + 4 × C–H single bonds; no lone pairs |
| Shape / notes | Planar (flat) molecule; unsaturated hydrocarbon |
| State at r.t.p. | Colourless gas |

## Physical Properties

- A simple molecular gas with very low melting and boiling points — only weak forces act between molecules ([[Intermolecular Force]]); the strong covalent bonds are not broken on boiling.
- Colourless, almost odourless, and insoluble in water.
- Does not conduct electricity — no mobile [[Ion|ions]] or [[Delocalised Electron|delocalised electrons]].

## Test for Unsaturation

Shake the gas with **bromine water** (orange). Ethene decolourises it rapidly because bromine **adds across the C=C double bond**:

C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂ (1,2-dibromoethane, colourless)

Saturated hydrocarbons such as the [[Alkanes]] leave bromine water orange (no reaction in the dark). This is the standard test in [[Chemical Tests]] for distinguishing alkenes from alkanes.

## Uses and Reactions

- **Addition polymerisation** — many ethene molecules join to form poly(ethene), used for bags and bottles (see [[Polymers]]).
- **Hydration** — ethene + steam over a phosphoric acid catalyst makes **ethanol** (see [[Alcohols]]).
- **Hydrogenation** — addition of H₂ converts unsaturated compounds to saturated ones (used to harden vegetable oils).
- Ethene is produced industrially by **cracking** larger hydrocarbon molecules from petroleum (see [[Fuels and Petroleum]]).

## Common Misconceptions

| Misconception | Reality |
|---|---|
| A double bond means two separate bonds that work independently. | The C=C double bond is **two shared pairs (4 electrons)** between the same two atoms, acting together as one double bond. |
| A double bond is exactly twice as strong as a single bond. | A C=C bond is stronger than a C–C bond but **not** twice as strong; part of it breaks readily in addition reactions, which is why alkenes are reactive. |
| Ethene turns bromine water orange. | Bromine water is **already orange**; ethene **decolourises** it (orange → colourless). |
| Ethene is unreactive like other hydrocarbons. | The C=C double bond makes ethene **unsaturated and reactive** — it undergoes addition reactions that [[Alkanes]] cannot. |

# Reactivity of Metals

**Summary**: The reactivity series orders metals by tendency to form positive ions: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > (C) > Zn > Fe > (H) > Cu > Ag > Au. More reactive metals displace less reactive ones from their compounds. Reaction with water/steam and dilute acids determines the order.

## The Reactivity Series

The reactivity series orders metals by their tendency to form positive ions. A more reactive metal loses electrons more readily than a less reactive one.

**Full reactivity series:**

| Metal | Symbol | Key Reactions |
|-------|--------|--------------|
| Potassium | K | Reacts violently with cold water; never found native |
| Sodium | Na | Reacts vigorously with cold water |
| Calcium | Ca | Reacts with cold water |
| Magnesium | Mg | Reacts slowly with cold water; reacts with steam |
| Aluminium | Al | Reacts with steam (if oxide layer removed) |
| **Carbon** | **(C)** | **Reducing agent benchmark** |
| Zinc | Zn | Reacts slowly with steam; displaces less reactive metals |
| Iron | Fe | Reacts slowly with steam; rusts in moist air |
| **Hydrogen** | **(H)** | **Acid reaction benchmark** |
| Copper | Cu | No reaction with acids or water |
| Silver | Ag | Found native; very unreactive |
| Gold | Au | Found native; does not tarnish |

Carbon and hydrogen are included as reference points. Carbon separates metals extracted by electrolysis from those extracted by reduction with carbon. Hydrogen separates metals that react with acids from those that do not.

## Determining the Reactivity Series

The reactivity series is determined experimentally by observing three types of reaction:

**(a) Reaction with water**

- **Potassium (K)**: Reacts violently with cold water -- melts, lilac flame, darts across surface.
  - 2K(s) + 2H2O(l) -> 2KOH(aq) + H2(g)
- **Sodium (Na)**: Reacts vigorously -- melts into a silver ball, fizzes rapidly.
  - 2Na(s) + 2H2O(l) -> 2NaOH(aq) + H2(g)
- **Calcium (Ca)**: Reacts steadily -- bubbles of H2, solution turns milky (Ca(OH)2 is sparingly soluble).
  - Ca(s) + 2H2O(l) -> Ca(OH)2(aq) + H2(g)
- **Magnesium (Mg)**: Reacts very slowly with cold water (few bubbles). Reacts vigorously with steam.
  - Mg(s) + H2O(g) -> MgO(s) + H2(g)
- **Aluminium (Al)**: No reaction with cold water (Al2O3 layer protects it). Reacts with steam if oxide layer is removed.
  - 2Al(s) + 3H2O(g) -> Al2O3(s) + 3H2(g)
- **Zinc (Zn)**: Reacts slowly with steam.
  - Zn(s) + H2O(g) -> ZnO(s) + H2(g)
- **Iron (Fe)**: Reacts very slowly with steam.
  - 3Fe(s) + 4H2O(g) -> Fe3O4(s) + 4H2(g)
- **Copper, Silver, Gold**: No reaction with water or steam.

**(b) Reaction with dilute HCl / H2SO4**

Metals **above hydrogen** in the reactivity series react with dilute acids to produce a **salt + hydrogen gas**. The more reactive the metal, the **more vigorous** the reaction.

- Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) -> MgCl2(aq) + H2(g)  (vigorous, rapid effervescence)
- Zn(s) + 2HCl(aq) -> ZnCl2(aq) + H2(g)  (steady effervescence)
- Fe(s) + 2HCl(aq) -> FeCl2(aq) + H2(g)  (slow effervescence)
- Cu(s) + HCl(aq) -> **no reaction** (copper is below hydrogen)
- Ag(s) + HCl(aq) -> **no reaction**
- Au(s) + HCl(aq) -> **no reaction**

With sulfuric acid, the salt formed is the sulfate:
- Mg(s) + H2SO4(aq) -> MgSO4(aq) + H2(g)

Group 1 metals (K, Na) are NOT tested with acids -- the reaction is dangerously explosive.

**(c) Displacement reactions**

A **more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal** from its compound (salt solution or oxide).

**Key example -- zinc and copper(II) sulfate:**
- Zn(s) + CuSO4(aq) -> ZnSO4(aq) + Cu(s)
- **Observations**: Blue solution fades (eventually becomes colourless), brown/pink deposit of copper metal forms on the zinc. Heat is released (exothermic reaction).
- **Explanation**: Zn atoms lose electrons more readily than Cu atoms. Zn -> Zn2+ + 2e- (oxidation), Cu2+ + 2e- -> Cu (reduction). This is a redox reaction.

**Other examples:**
- Fe(s) + CuSO4(aq) -> FeSO4(aq) + Cu(s)  (iron is more reactive than copper -- reaction occurs)
- Cu(s) + ZnSO4(aq) -> **no reaction** (copper is less reactive than zinc -- cannot displace it)
- Mg(s) + ZnSO4(aq) -> MgSO4(aq) + Zn(s)  (magnesium is more reactive than zinc)
- Zn(s) + FeSO4(aq) -> ZnSO4(aq) + Fe(s)  (zinc is more reactive than iron)

**Displacement of metal oxides:**
Carbon is used to extract metals below it in the reactivity series by heating the metal oxide with carbon:
- 2Fe2O3(s) + 3C(s) -> 4Fe(l) + 3CO2(g)  (iron extraction -- carbon reduces iron(III) oxide)
- ZnO(s) + C(s) -> Zn(g) + CO(g)  (zinc extraction)
- Al2O3(s) + C(s) -> **no reaction** (aluminium is above carbon in the series -- electrolysis is required)

## The Thermite Reaction

The thermite reaction is a highly exothermic displacement reaction between aluminium and iron(III) oxide:

**2Al(s) + Fe2O3(s) -> Al2O3(s) + 2Fe(l)**

Key points:
- Aluminium is more reactive than iron, so it displaces iron from its oxide.
- The reaction is **extremely exothermic** (temperatures reach ~2500 degrees C).
- The iron produced is **molten** -- it flows into gaps.
- **Use**: welding railway tracks together. The molten iron fills the gap between rails and solidifies to join them.
- Magnesium ribbon is used as a fuse to ignite the mixture (high activation energy).

## Carbon as a Reducing Agent

Carbon is a non-metal but is included in the reactivity series because it is used as a **reducing agent** in metal extraction.

- Carbon can reduce oxides of metals **BELOW** it in the reactivity series (ZnO, Fe2O3, CuO):
  - CuO(s) + C(s) -> Cu(s) + CO(g)
- Carbon **CANNOT** reduce oxides of metals **ABOVE** it:
  - Al2O3 requires **electrolysis** because aluminium is more reactive than carbon.

## Practical: Determining Order of Reactivity

**Method** (using temperature rise with copper(II) sulfate):
1. Pour a known volume of copper(II) sulfate solution into a polystyrene cup.
2. Record the initial temperature.
3. Add a measured mass of the metal (powder or turnings to increase surface area).
4. Stir and record the **maximum temperature** reached.
5. Calculate the **temperature rise** for each metal.
6. The **greater the temperature rise**, the **more reactive** the metal.

**Alternative method** (observational):
- Add equal-sized pieces of different metals to equal volumes and concentrations of dilute HCl.
- Observe the **rate of hydrogen production** (bubbles per unit time).
- More vigorous fizzing = more reactive metal.

**Control variables**: mass of metal, surface area, volume and concentration of solution, initial temperature.

## Key Points

- Reactivity series: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > (C) > Zn > Fe > (H) > Cu > Ag > Au
- More reactive metal = loses electrons more readily = forms positive ions more easily
- Metals above H react with dilute acids to form salt + H2
- More reactive metal + compound of less reactive metal -> displacement reaction
- Thermite: 2Al + Fe2O3 -> Al2O3 + 2Fe (highly exothermic, welding rails)
- Carbon reduces oxides of metals BELOW it; electrolysis needed for metals ABOVE it
- Reaction with water is increasingly vigorous going up the series (K, Na, Ca with cold water; Mg, Al, Zn, Fe with steam)
- Blue CuSO4 solution goes colourless when Cu2+ is displaced by a more reactive metal

## Key Concepts from Past Papers

- **Reactivity series**: the order of metals by their tendency to form positive ions/lose electrons
- **Displacement reaction**: a reaction in which a more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its compound
- **Reducing agent**: a substance that reduces another substance (and is itself oxidised)
- Zinc is more reactive than copper, so zinc displaces copper from copper sulfate
- Blue colour fades because Cu2+ ions are removed from solution
- Carbon can only reduce metal oxides of metals below it in the reactivity series

## Common Misconceptions

| Misconception | Reality |
|---|---|
| "All metals react with acids" | Only metals ABOVE hydrogen in the reactivity series react with dilute acids. Copper, silver, and gold do not react. |
| "Carbon is a metal" | Carbon is a non-metal placed in the reactivity series as a reference point for reduction reactions. |
| "The thermite reaction is used to make aluminium" | The thermite reaction produces IRON from iron(III) oxide using aluminium. It does not produce aluminium. |
| "Displacement always means one metal replaces another from a solution" | Displacement can also occur with solid metal oxides (e.g., thermite reaction, carbon reduction). |
| "A more reactive metal always reacts faster" | Rate also depends on surface area, concentration, and temperature. Sodium reacts faster than calcium despite both being very reactive. |

# States of Matter

**Summary**: Matter exists in three states: solid, liquid, and gas. The kinetic particle theory explains their properties in terms of particle arrangement, movement, and energy.

## The Three States of Matter

| Property | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
|----------|-------|--------|-----|
| Particle arrangement | Regular, fixed positions | Random, touching | Random, far apart |
| Particle movement | Vibrate in place | Slide past each other | Move rapidly in all directions |
| Shape | Fixed | Takes shape of container (below surface) | Fills container completely |
| Volume | Fixed | Fixed | Not fixed (compressible) |
| Density | High | Medium | Low |
| Compressibility | Very low | Very low | High |

## Kinetic Particle Theory

All matter is made of tiny particles in constant motion. The temperature of a substance is a measure of the average kinetic energy of its particles.

- **Heating** increases particle kinetic energy → particles move faster → can overcome attractive forces
- **Cooling** decreases kinetic energy → particles move slower → attractive forces pull particles closer

## Changes of State

```
Solid ⇌ Liquid ⇌ Gas
  (melting/freezing)  (boiling/condensation)
```

- **Melting**: Solid → Liquid (at melting point, energy breaks lattice)
- **Freezing**: Liquid → Solid (at freezing point)
- **Boiling/Evaporation**: Liquid → Gas (at boiling point, or below via evaporation)
- **Condensation**: Gas → Liquid
- **Sublimation**: Solid → Gas directly (e.g., CO₂ dry ice, iodine)
- **Deposition**: Gas → Solid directly

**Key difference — boiling vs evaporation:**
- Boiling occurs at a specific temperature (boiling point) throughout the liquid
- Evaporation occurs at any temperature, only at the surface

## Heating and Cooling Curves

During a change of state, the temperature stays **constant** even though heating continues — the energy is used to overcome inter-particle forces (latent heat), not to increase kinetic energy.

## Diffusion

Diffusion is the net movement of particles from an area of higher concentration to lower concentration due to random particle motion. Gases diffuse fastest, liquids slower, solids extremely slowly.

**Factors affecting diffusion rate:**
- Temperature (higher = faster, particles have more KE)
- Particle mass (lighter particles diffuse faster)
- Concentration gradient (steeper = faster)

## Key Facts to Memorize

- During a state change, temperature remains constant — energy goes into breaking bonds, not raising temperature
- Solids have the strongest inter-particle forces; gases have the weakest
- Diffusion provides evidence for the kinetic particle theory
- Brownian motion (random movement of smoke/pollen grains in water) is caused by collisions with invisible water particles
- Gases can be compressed because there is space between particles

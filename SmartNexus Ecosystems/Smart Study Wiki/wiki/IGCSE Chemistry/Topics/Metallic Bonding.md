# Metallic Bonding

**Summary**: Electrostatic attraction between a lattice of positive metal ions and a sea of delocalised electrons. Explains malleability, conductivity, and high melting points.
**Tags**: #igcse #chemistry #bonding
**Created**: 2026-07-14
**Last Updated**: 2026-07-14

---

## Structure

- **Lattice of positive metal ions** — arranged in regular layers
- **Sea of delocalised electrons** — outer shell electrons that are free to move throughout the structure
- The bond = **electrostatic attraction** between positive ions and the delocalised electrons

## Properties Explained

| Property | Explanation |
|----------|------------|
| **High melting/boiling points** | Strong electrostatic attraction between positive ions and delocalised electrons requires a lot of energy to overcome |
| **Good electrical conductivity** | Delocalised electrons are free to move and carry charge through the structure |
| **Good thermal conductivity** | Delocalised electrons transfer kinetic/heat energy rapidly |
| **Malleable and ductile** | Layers of ions can slide over each other without breaking the metallic bonds — the delocalised electrons move with the ions and continue to hold them together |
| **Shiny/lustrous** | Delocalised electrons reflect light |

## Alloys

An **alloy** is stronger/harder than a pure metal because:
- Different-sized atoms disrupt the regular layers
- Layers can no longer slide over each other easily
- This is why alloys are preferred for structural uses

## Key Facts
- Metallic bond = attraction between positive metal ions and delocalised electrons
- Delocalised electrons = outer shell electrons free to move through the lattice
- Malleable because layers slide (bonding is non-directional, unlike ionic/covalent)
- Alloys are harder than pure metals

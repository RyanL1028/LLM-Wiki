## Method for Reacting Mass Calculations

1. Write the **balanced equation**
2. Find the **moles** of the known substance: n = m / Mr
3. Use the **mole ratio** from the balanced equation
4. Find the **mass** of the unknown: m = n × Mr

## Worked Example

**What mass of CO₂ is produced when 50g of CaCO₃ decomposes?**

CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂
- Mr(CaCO₃) = 100; Mr(CO₂) = 44
- Moles of CaCO₃ = 50/100 = 0.5 mol
- Mole ratio CaCO₃ : CO₂ = 1 : 1 → 0.5 mol CO₂
- Mass of CO₂ = 0.5 × 44 = **22 g**

## Percentage Yield

% yield = (actual yield / theoretical yield) × 100

**Reasons for less than 100% yield:**
- Reaction may be reversible (doesn't go to completion)
- Side reactions produce other products
- Product lost during separation/purification (filtration, crystallisation)
- Reactants may be impure

## Limiting Reactant

The reactant that is **completely used up first** — it limits the amount of product formed. The other reactant is in **excess**.

To identify the limiting reactant:
1. Calculate moles of each reactant
2. Compare with the mole ratio in the balanced equation
3. The one that runs out first is the limiting reactant

## Key Facts
- ALWAYS start with a balanced equation
- Moles = mass / Mr — memorize this
- Use mole ratio from the balanced equation coefficients
- Actual yield < theoretical yield is normal

# Reactivity of Metals

**Summary**: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > (H) > Cu > Ag > Au. More reactive metals displace less reactive ones. Reactions with water, steam, and acids follow the reactivity order.

## The Reactivity Series

| Metal | Reaction with cold water | Reaction with steam | Reaction with dilute acid |
|-------|------------------------|---------------------|--------------------------|
| Potassium (K) | Violent, ignites, H₂ evolved | — | Explosive (too dangerous) |
| Sodium (Na) | Vigorous, melts, darts | — | Explosive |
| Calcium (Ca) | Reacts steadily, bubbles | — | Vigorous |
| Magnesium (Mg) | Very slow / no reaction | Reacts vigorously, bright white flame | Reacts vigorously, H₂ evolved |
| Aluminium (Al) | No reaction (oxide layer) | Reacts (oxide layer removed) | Reacts (oxide layer removed) |
| Zinc (Zn) | No reaction | Reacts when heated | Reacts, H₂ evolved |
| Iron (Fe) | No reaction | Reacts when heated | Reacts slowly |
| Lead (Pb) | No reaction | No reaction | Reacts very slowly with warm acid |
| Copper (Cu) | No reaction | No reaction | No reaction |
| Silver (Ag) | No reaction | No reaction | No reaction |
| Gold (Au) | No reaction | No reaction | No reaction |

## Displacement Reactions

A **more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal** from its compound.

- Zn(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + Cu(s) — zinc is MORE reactive than copper
- Cu(s) + ZnSO₄(aq) → NO REACTION — copper is LESS reactive than zinc
- Mg(s) + FeSO₄(aq) → MgSO₄(aq) + Fe(s)

## Reaction with Water

**General equation**: Metal + Water → Metal Hydroxide + Hydrogen

- 2Na(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H₂(g)

## Reaction with Acid

**General equation**: Metal + Acid → Salt + Hydrogen

- Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂(g)
- Only metals ABOVE hydrogen react with acids to produce H₂

## Key Facts
- Reactivity = tendency of a metal to lose electrons and form positive ions
- More reactive → loses electrons more easily
- Carbon is used as a reducing agent to extract metals BELOW it in the series
- Metals above carbon must be extracted by electrolysis

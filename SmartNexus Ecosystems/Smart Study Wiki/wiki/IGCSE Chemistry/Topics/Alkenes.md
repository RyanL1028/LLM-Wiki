# Alkenes

**Summary**: Unsaturated hydrocarbons with C=C double bond: CₙH₂ₙ. Ethene (C₂H₄) is the simplest. Undergo addition reactions (Br₂, H₂, H₂O) and addition polymerisation.

## General Formula: CₙH₂ₙ

Alkenes are **unsaturated hydrocarbons** — they contain at least one C=C double bond.

## The C=C Double Bond

- One sigma (σ) bond + one pi (π) bond
- The double bond is the **functional group**
- It is an area of high electron density → susceptible to **electrophilic attack** (addition reactions)
- Alkenes are more reactive than alkanes

## Key Alkenes

| Name | Formula | Structure |
|------|---------|-----------|
| Ethene | C₂H₄ | CH₂=CH₂ |
| Propene | C₃H₆ | CH₃CH=CH₂ |
| Butene | C₄H₈ | Several isomers |

## Addition Reactions

The double bond **opens up** and atoms add across it → a single saturated product.

### 1. Bromination (Test for Unsaturation)
C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂
- Bromine water changes from **orange/brown → colourless**
- This is THE test for an unsaturated compound (C=C)
- Alkanes do NOT decolourise bromine water (no C=C)

### 2. Hydrogenation
C₂H₄ + H₂ → C₂H₆
- Requires Ni catalyst, ~150°C
- Used to harden vegetable oils → margarine

### 3. Hydration (Addition of Steam)
C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH
- Requires H₃PO₄ catalyst, 300°C, 60 atm
- Industrial method for ethanol production

### 4. Addition Polymerisation
n(CH₂=CH₂) → —(CH₂—CH₂)—ₙ
- Many ethene molecules join to form poly(ethene)
- The double bond opens up — no other products formed

## Cracking

Long-chain alkanes are broken into shorter alkanes + alkenes by **cracking**:
- **Catalytic cracking**: Al₂O₃ catalyst, ~550°C
- **Thermal cracking**: very high temperature and pressure
- Produces a mixture of alkanes AND alkenes

## Key Facts
- Alkenes are UNSATURATED (C=C) — they DO decolourise bromine water
- Addition reaction: the double bond opens and atoms join on
- Ethene → poly(ethene): addition polymerisation
- Cracking produces alkenes from long alkanes

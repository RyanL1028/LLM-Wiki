# Diffusion

**Summary**: The net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to lower concentration, resulting from random particle motion.

**Created**: 2026-07-14

## Definition

**Diffusion** is the net movement of particles from an area of **higher concentration** to an area of **lower concentration** down a concentration gradient. It occurs because particles are in constant random motion.

## Factors Affecting Rate of Diffusion

### Temperature
Higher temperature → particles have more kinetic energy → move faster → faster diffusion

### Particle Mass / Molecular Mass
Lighter particles diffuse faster than heavier particles at the same temperature.
- NH₃ (Mr = 17) diffuses faster than HCl (Mr = 36.5)
- This is demonstrated in the classic NH₃ + HCl experiment

### Concentration Gradient
A steeper concentration gradient → faster rate of diffusion

### State of Matter
- **Gases**: Fastest diffusion (particles move freely, large spaces)
- **Liquids**: Slower (particles closer together)
- **Solids**: Extremely slow (particles fixed in position)

## Classic Demonstration: NH₃ + HCl → NH₄Cl

- Cotton wool soaked in ammonia solution at one end of a glass tube
- Cotton wool soaked in hydrochloric acid at the other end
- A **white ring of ammonium chloride** (NH₄Cl) forms where the two gases meet
- The ring forms **closer to the HCl end** because NH₃ (lighter, Mr=17) diffuses faster than HCl (heavier, Mr=36.5)

## Brownian Motion

Observed when pollen grains or smoke particles are suspended in water/air and viewed under a microscope:
- Particles move in a random, jerky (zigzag) path
- Caused by **collisions with invisible, fast-moving water/air molecules**
- Provides evidence for the kinetic particle theory

## Key Facts to Memorize

- Diffusion is driven by random particle motion, not external forces
- Lighter gas particles diffuse faster than heavier ones at the same temperature
- The white ring in the NH₃/HCl experiment forms closer to the HCl end
- Diffusion does NOT occur in solids in any practical timeframe
- Diffusion rate increases with temperature

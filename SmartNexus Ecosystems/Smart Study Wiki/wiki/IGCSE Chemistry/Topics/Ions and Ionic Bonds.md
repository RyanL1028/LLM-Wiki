# Ions and Ionic Bonds

**Summary**: Ionic bonding: electron transfer from metal to non-metal, forming oppositely charged ions held in a giant ionic lattice by strong electrostatic forces.

## How Ionic Bonds Form

1. Metal atom **loses** electron(s) → positive ion (cation)
2. Non-metal atom **gains** electron(s) → negative ion (anion)
3. Oppositely charged ions attract each other → **electrostatic attraction** = ionic bond

## Example: NaCl

- Na (2.8.1) → Na⁺ (2.8) + e⁻ — sodium loses 1 electron
- Cl (2.8.7) + e⁻ → Cl⁻ (2.8.8) — chlorine gains 1 electron
- Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → NaCl — electrostatic attraction forms the ionic bond

## Giant Ionic Lattice

Ionic compounds form a **giant ionic lattice** — a regular 3D arrangement of alternating positive and negative ions.

## Properties of Ionic Compounds

| Property | Explanation |
|----------|------------|
| **High melting/boiling points** | Strong electrostatic forces between ions require a lot of energy to overcome |
| **Conduct electricity when molten/aqueous** | Ions are free to move and carry charge |
| **Do NOT conduct when solid** | Ions are fixed in the lattice — cannot move |
| **Brittle** | When force is applied, layers shift and like charges repel → crystal shatters |
| **Soluble in water** (many) | Water molecules surround and separate the ions |

## Key Facts
- Ionic bond = electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions
- Metal → loses electrons → cation
- Non-metal → gains electrons → anion
- Giant ionic lattice → high melting point, conducts only when molten/aqueous

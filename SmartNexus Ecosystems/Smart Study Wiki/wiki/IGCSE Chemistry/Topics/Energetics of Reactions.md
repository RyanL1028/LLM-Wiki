# Energetics of Reactions

**Summary**: Exothermic reactions release heat (ΔH negative); endothermic reactions absorb heat (ΔH positive). Bond breaking is endothermic; bond making is exothermic.
**Tags**: #igcse #chemistry #energetics
**Created**: 2026-07-14

## Exothermic Reactions
Reactions that **release thermal energy** to the surroundings. The temperature of the surroundings increases.

**Examples:**
- Combustion: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O (+ heat)
- Neutralisation: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O (+ heat)
- Respiration: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O (+ energy)
- Displacement: Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu (+ heat)
- Adding water to anhydrous copper(II) sulfate (white → blue)

## Endothermic Reactions
Reactions that **absorb thermal energy** from the surroundings. The temperature of the surroundings decreases.

**Examples:**
- Thermal decomposition: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ (requires heating)
- Photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (absorbs light energy)
- Dissolving ammonium nitrate in water (gets cold)
- Reaction of citric acid with sodium hydrogencarbonate

## Energy Level Diagrams

### Exothermic:
- Products have **less energy** than reactants
- ΔH is **negative**
- Arrow goes DOWN from reactants to products
- Activation energy (Ea) is the minimum energy needed to start the reaction

### Endothermic:
- Products have **more energy** than reactants
- ΔH is **positive**
- Arrow goes UP from reactants to products

## Bond Energies
- **Bond breaking** is ENDOTHERMIC (requires energy input)
- **Bond making** is EXOTHERMIC (releases energy)
- ΔH = Σ(bond energies of bonds broken) − Σ(bond energies of bonds formed)
- If bonds broken > bonds formed → endothermic (ΔH positive)
- If bonds formed > bonds broken → exothermic (ΔH negative)

## Key Facts
- Exothermic: surroundings get HOTTER, ΔH negative
- Endothermic: surroundings get COLDER, ΔH positive
- Activation energy (Ea) is the minimum collision energy for a successful reaction
- Catalysts lower the activation energy (provide an alternative pathway)

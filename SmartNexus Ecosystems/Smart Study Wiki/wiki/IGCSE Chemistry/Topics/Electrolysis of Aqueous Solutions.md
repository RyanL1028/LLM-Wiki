# Electrolysis of Aqueous Solutions

**Summary**: In aqueous electrolysis, water can be oxidized/reduced in competition with dissolved ions. The products depend on the reactivity of the metal and the anion present.

## Discharge Rules — Cathode (−)

At the cathode, the ion that is **less reactive** is discharged:

| Cations present | Discharged at cathode | Product |
|----------------|----------------------|---------|
| K⁺, Na⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺ | H⁺ (from water) | H₂ gas |
| Zn²⁺, Fe²⁺, Pb²⁺, Cu²⁺, Ag⁺ | The metal ion | Metal deposited |

**Why?** Highly reactive metals hold onto their electrons strongly, so H⁺ from water is easier to reduce.

## Discharge Rules — Anode (+)

| Anions present | Discharged at anode | Product |
|---------------|---------------------|---------|
| Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻ (halides) | Halide ion | Cl₂, Br₂, I₂ |
| SO₄²⁻, NO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻ | OH⁻ (from water) | O₂ gas |

**Concentration matters**: In **concentrated** NaCl(aq), Cl⁻ is discharged (Cl₂ gas). In **dilute** NaCl(aq), OH⁻ may be discharged instead (O₂ gas).

## Key Examples

### Electrolysis of Concentrated NaCl(aq) (Brine)
- Cathode (−): 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂(g) — hydrogen gas
- Anode (+): 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂(g) + 2e⁻ — chlorine gas
- Solution becomes NaOH(aq) as Na⁺ and OH⁻ remain behind

### Electrolysis of CuSO₄(aq) with Carbon Electrodes
- Cathode (−): Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu(s) — brown/pink copper metal deposits
- Anode (+): 4OH⁻ → O₂(g) + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ — oxygen gas evolved
- Blue colour of solution fades as Cu²⁺ ions are removed

### Electrolysis of Dilute H₂SO₄(aq)
- This is effectively the electrolysis of water
- Cathode (−): H₂ gas (from H⁺)
- Anode (+): O₂ gas (from OH⁻)
- Volume of H₂ : O₂ = 2 : 1

## Tests for Products
- **H₂**: Lighted splint → squeaky pop
- **O₂**: Glowing splint → relights
- **Cl₂**: Damp blue litmus → red then bleaches white
- **Metal deposit**: Observe colour (Cu = brown/pink)

## Key Facts
- CATIONS → CATHODE (reduction); ANIONS → ANODE (oxidation)
- H₂ produced at cathode if the metal is more reactive than hydrogen
- Halides produce halogen at anode; otherwise O₂ from OH⁻
- In concentrated halide solutions, the halide is discharged; in dilute, O₂ may be preferred

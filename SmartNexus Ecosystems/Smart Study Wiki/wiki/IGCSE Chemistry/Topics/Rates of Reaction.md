# Rates of Reaction

**Summary**: Reaction rate depends on concentration, temperature, surface area, and catalysts. Explained by collision theory.

## Collision Theory

For a reaction to occur, particles must:
1. **Collide** with each other
2. Have **sufficient energy** (≥ activation energy)
3. Have the **correct orientation**

Anything that increases the **frequency of successful collisions** increases the rate of reaction.

## Factors Affecting Rate

### 1. Concentration (of reactants in solution)
- Higher concentration → more particles per unit volume → more frequent collisions → **faster rate**
- For gases, increasing pressure has the same effect

### 2. Temperature
- Higher temperature → particles have more kinetic energy → move faster
- Two effects: (a) more frequent collisions, (b) more particles have energy ≥ activation energy
- The increased energy effect is MORE important than increased collision frequency

### 3. Surface Area (of solid reactants)
- Smaller particle size / greater surface area → more particles exposed for reaction
- Powdered solids react faster than large lumps
- Example: powdered CaCO₃ reacts faster with acid than marble chips

### 4. Catalyst
- A substance that **increases the rate** without being chemically changed or used up
- Provides an **alternative reaction pathway** with **lower activation energy**
- Does NOT change the position of equilibrium (for reversible reactions)
- Does NOT affect the yield — only the rate
- Examples: MnO₂ for H₂O₂ decomposition; Fe in Haber process; V₂O₅ in Contact process

### 5. Pressure (for gases)
- Higher pressure → gas particles closer together → more frequent collisions → faster rate

## Measuring Rate of Reaction

Rate can be measured by:
- Volume of gas produced per unit time (gas syringe or inverted measuring cylinder)
- Change in mass per unit time (if gas escapes — mass balance)
- Time for a cross to disappear (precipitation reactions)
- Colour change / pH change

## Interpreting Rate Graphs
- Steeper slope = faster reaction
- Curve flattens when reaction is complete (limiting reactant used up)
- Same total amount of product with/without catalyst (catalyst only changes rate)

## Key Facts
- Rate ∝ collision frequency × fraction of collisions with E ≥ Ea
- Temperature increase of 10°C approximately doubles the rate
- Catalysts are NOT used up and are chemically unchanged
- Catalysts lower Ea — they do NOT change ΔH

# Alkanes

**Summary**: Saturated hydrocarbons: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂. Methane (CH₄), ethane (C₂H₆), propane (C₃H₈), butane (C₄H₁₀). Undergo combustion and substitution with halogens (UV light).

## General Formula: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂

Alkanes are **saturated hydrocarbons** — they contain only single C—C and C—H bonds. No double or triple bonds.

## First Four Alkanes

| Name | Formula | Mr | Boiling point | State at r.t.p. |
|------|---------|-----|---------------|-----------------|
| Methane | CH₄ | 16 | −162°C | Gas |
| Ethane | C₂H₆ | 30 | −89°C | Gas |
| Propane | C₃H₈ | 44 | −42°C | Gas |
| Butane | C₄H₁₀ | 58 | −1°C | Gas |

## Reactions

### 1. Combustion

**Complete combustion (excess O₂):**
CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

**Incomplete combustion (limited O₂):**
2CH₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO + 4H₂O
- Produces carbon monoxide (CO) — toxic, colourless, odourless
- May also produce carbon (soot) — CH₄ + O₂ → C + 2H₂O

### 2. Substitution with Halogens

Alkanes react with halogens (Cl₂, Br₂) in the presence of **UV light** (sunlight).

CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl (chloromethane)
- One hydrogen atom is **substituted** by a halogen atom
- This is a **photochemical reaction** — requires UV light to break the Cl—Cl bond
- Further substitution can occur: CH₃Cl + Cl₂ → CH₂Cl₂ + HCl etc.

## Trends
- Boiling point **increases** as chain length increases (stronger intermolecular forces)
- Viscosity **increases** with chain length
- Flammability **decreases** with chain length

## Sources
- Mainly from **petroleum** (crude oil) and **natural gas**
- Separated by **fractional distillation**

## Key Facts
- Alkanes are SATURATED (only single bonds) — they do NOT decolourise bromine water
- Complete combustion: alkane + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O
- Substitution requires UV LIGHT
- General formula: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂

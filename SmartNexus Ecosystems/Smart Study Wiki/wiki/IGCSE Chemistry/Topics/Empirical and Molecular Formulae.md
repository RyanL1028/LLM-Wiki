# Empirical and Molecular Formulae

**Summary**: Empirical formula = simplest whole-number ratio of atoms. Molecular formula = actual number of atoms in a molecule.

**Created**: 2026-07-14

## Empirical Formula

The **simplest whole-number ratio** of atoms of each element in a compound.

### How to Calculate
1. Write the mass or percentage of each element
2. Divide each by the element's Ar → gives mole ratio
3. Divide all by the smallest number → gives simplest ratio
4. Write the formula

**Worked example**: A compound contains 40% Ca, 12% C, 48% O. Find its empirical formula.
- Ca: 40/40 = 1.0
- C: 12/12 = 1.0
- O: 48/16 = 3.0
- Ratio = 1 : 1 : 3 → **CaCO₃**

## Molecular Formula

The **actual number of atoms** of each element in one molecule.

Molecular formula = (Empirical formula) × n
where n = Mr / empirical formula mass

## Percentage Composition

% of element = (total Ar of that element in formula / Mr of compound) × 100

**Example**: % of O in H₂O
- Mr(H₂O) = 18
- %O = (16/18) × 100 = 88.9%

## Key Facts
- Empirical formula is always the simplest ratio — it may or may not equal the molecular formula
- For ionic compounds, the formula given is always the empirical formula
- To find molecular formula, you need BOTH the empirical formula AND the Mr

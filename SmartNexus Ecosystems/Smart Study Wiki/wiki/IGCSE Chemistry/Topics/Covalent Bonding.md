# Covalent Bonding

**Summary**: Covalent bonding: sharing of electron pairs between non-metal atoms. Simple molecular structures (low mp) or giant covalent networks (diamond, graphite, SiO₂ — high mp).

## How Covalent Bonds Form

Non-metal atoms **share** pairs of electrons to achieve a full outer shell (noble gas configuration). Each shared pair = one covalent bond.

## Simple Molecular Structures

### Examples: H₂, Cl₂, O₂, H₂O, CH₄, CO₂, NH₃

| Substance | Formula | Bonds | Shape |
|-----------|---------|-------|-------|
| Water | H₂O | 2 × O—H | Bent (V-shaped) |
| Methane | CH₄ | 4 × C—H | Tetrahedral |
| Carbon dioxide | CO₂ | 2 × C=O | Linear |
| Ammonia | NH₃ | 3 × N—H | Trigonal pyramidal |
| Oxygen | O₂ | 1 × O=O | Linear |

### Properties
- **Low melting/boiling points** — weak intermolecular forces (not the strong covalent bonds) are overcome
- **Do NOT conduct electricity** — no mobile ions or free electrons
- **Often gases or liquids** at r.t.p.

## Giant Covalent Structures

### Diamond (carbon)
- Each C atom bonded to **4 others** (tetrahedral)
- **Very hard** — strongest known natural material
- **Very high melting point** — many strong covalent bonds
- **Does NOT conduct** — all electrons fixed in bonds
- Used for cutting tools, jewellery

### Graphite (carbon)
- Each C atom bonded to **3 others** (hexagonal layers)
- **Soft/slippery** — layers slide over each other (weak forces between layers)
- **Conducts electricity** — each C has one delocalised electron
- **Very high melting point** — strong covalent bonds within layers
- Used for pencils, lubricants, electrodes

### Silicon Dioxide (SiO₂ / silica)
- Similar structure to diamond (each Si bonded to 4 O; each O bonded to 2 Si)
- **Very hard, very high melting point**
- Used in glass, ceramics, sand

## Key Facts
- Covalent bond = shared pair of electrons
- Simple molecular = strong covalent bonds WITHIN molecules, WEAK intermolecular forces BETWEEN molecules
- Low mp of simple molecular substances is due to weak intermolecular forces, NOT weak covalent bonds
- Diamond vs graphite: same element, different structure = **allotropes**

# Composition of Air

**Summary**: Clean dry air: ~78% N₂, ~21% O₂, ~0.9% Ar, ~0.04% CO₂. Oxygen percentage can be measured by reaction with heated copper or phosphorus.

## Composition of Clean Dry Air

| Gas | Percentage |
|-----|-----------|
| Nitrogen (N₂) | ~78% |
| Oxygen (O₂) | ~21% |
| Argon (Ar) | ~0.9% |
| Carbon dioxide (CO₂) | ~0.04% |
| Other noble gases (Ne, He, Kr, Xe) | Trace |
| Water vapour | Variable (not included in "dry" air) |

## Determining the Percentage of Oxygen in Air

### Copper Method
- Pass air over heated copper in a silica tube
- 2Cu(s) + O₂(g) → 2CuO(s)
- Oxygen reacts with copper; volume of gas decreases
- % O₂ = (volume decrease / initial volume) × 100

### Phosphorus Method
- Burn phosphorus in a bell jar over water
- 4P(s) + 5O₂(g) → 2P₂O₅(s)
- Water rises to fill the volume previously occupied by oxygen
- % O₂ = (rise in water level / total height) × 100

### Iron Wool Method
- Damp iron wool in an inverted measuring cylinder over water
- 4Fe(s) + 3O₂(g) + 2nH₂O(l) → 2Fe₂O₃·nH₂O
- Iron rusts, consuming oxygen; water rises

## Key Facts
- Nitrogen makes up ~4/5 of the air
- Oxygen makes up ~1/5 of the air
- CO₂ is a trace gas (0.04%) but critically important for photosynthesis and climate
- Noble gases (mainly argon) make up ~1%

# Sulfur and Compounds

## Sources of Sulfur

- **Natural**: Volcanic eruptions, hot springs
- **Fossil fuels**: Coal, oil, and natural gas contain sulfur impurities
- **Sulfide ores**: e.g., ZnS (zinc blende), PbS (galena), CuFeS₂ (chalcopyrite)

## Production of SO₂

1. **Burning sulfur in air**: S(s) + O₂(g) → SO₂(g)
2. **Roasting sulfide ores**: 2ZnS(s) + 3O₂(g) → 2ZnO(s) + 2SO₂(g)

## The Contact Process (Making H₂SO₄)

### Step 1: SO₂ → SO₃
2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g)  (ΔH = −197 kJ/mol)

**Conditions:**
- **Catalyst**: Vanadium(V) oxide (V₂O₅)
- **Temperature**: ~450°C (compromise — low temp favours yield (exothermic), but reasonable rate needed)
- **Pressure**: ~2 atm (low pressure sufficient — equilibrium already favours SO₃)

### Step 2: SO₃ → H₂SO₄
SO₃ + H₂SO₄ → H₂S₂O₇ (oleum/disulfuric acid)
H₂S₂O₇ + H₂O → 2H₂SO₄

**Note**: SO₃ is NOT dissolved directly in water (reaction is too violent and forms a mist). Instead, it's dissolved in existing H₂SO₄ to form oleum, then diluted.

## Properties of Sulfuric Acid

- **Strong dibasic acid**: H₂SO₄ → 2H⁺ + SO₄²⁻
- **Dehydrating agent**: Removes water from substances (chars sugar, dehydrates CuSO₄·5H₂O → CuSO₄)
- **Oxidising agent** (when concentrated and hot)

## Key Facts
- Sulfur is a yellow solid at r.t.p.
- SO₂ is a colourless, pungent gas (also an air pollutant causing acid rain)
- Contact process catalyst: V₂O₅ (vanadium(V) oxide)
- H₂SO₄ is the most manufactured chemical globally

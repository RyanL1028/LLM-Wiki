# Electrolysis Applications

**Summary**: Industrial uses of electrolysis: aluminium extraction (Hall-Héroult), copper refining, electroplating, and brine electrolysis for NaOH production.

## 1. Extraction of Aluminium (Hall-Héroult Process)

Aluminium is extracted from bauxite (Al₂O₃) by electrolysis because aluminium is more reactive than carbon.

### Key Details:
- **Electrolyte**: Al₂O₃ dissolved in **molten cryolite** (Na₃AlF₆)
- **Why cryolite?** Lowers the melting point from ~2000°C to ~950°C, saving energy and cost
- **Electrodes**: Graphite (carbon)
- **Cathode (−)**: Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al(l) — molten aluminium collects at bottom
- **Anode (+)**: 2O²⁻ → O₂(g) + 4e⁻ — oxygen gas
- **Problem**: Oxygen reacts with graphite anodes: C + O₂ → CO₂ — anodes burn away and must be replaced regularly

## 2. Copper Refining (Purification)

Impure copper from smelting is purified electrolytically:
- **Anode (+)**: Impure copper — Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ (dissolves)
- **Cathode (−)**: Pure copper sheet — Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (deposits)
- **Electrolyte**: CuSO₄(aq)
- Impurities (Ag, Au, Pt) fall to the bottom as **anode sludge/slime**

## 3. Electroplating

Coating an object with a thin layer of metal (e.g., chromium, silver, nickel) for protection or appearance:
- **Anode (+)** = The plating metal (e.g., silver)
- **Cathode (−)** = The object to be plated
- **Electrolyte** = Solution containing ions of the plating metal
- Example: Silver plating uses Ag anode, object as cathode, AgNO₃(aq) electrolyte

## 4. Brine Electrolysis (Chlor-alkali Industry)

Electrolysis of concentrated NaCl(aq) produces three important chemicals:
- **Chlorine (Cl₂)** — water treatment, PVC, disinfectants, bleach
- **Hydrogen (H₂)** — ammonia production (Haber process), fuel, margarine
- **Sodium hydroxide (NaOH)** — soap, paper, textiles, drain cleaner

## Key Facts
- Cryolite reduces Al₂O₃ melting point from ~2000°C to ~950°C
- Graphite anodes in aluminium extraction are consumed (C + O₂ → CO₂)
- In copper refining, anode impurities form sludge; pure copper deposits at cathode
- Electroplating: plating metal = anode, object to be plated = cathode

# Relative Masses and Moles

**Summary**: Ar = relative atomic mass; Mr = relative formula/molecular mass. One mole = 6.02 × 10²³ particles. Molar mass = mass of 1 mole in grams (g/mol).

## Relative Atomic Mass (Ar)

Ar is the average mass of an atom of an element relative to 1/12th the mass of a carbon-12 atom. It appears on the Periodic Table.

## Relative Formula Mass (Mr) / Relative Molecular Mass

Mr = sum of Ar of all atoms in the formula.

**Examples:**
- H₂O: (2 × 1) + 16 = 18
- CaCO₃: 40 + 12 + (3 × 16) = 100
- Mg(OH)₂: 24 + 2×(16+1) = 58
- (NH₄)₂SO₄: 2×(14+4) + 32 + (4×16) = 132

## The Mole

**One mole** of any substance contains **6.02 × 10²³** particles (Avogadro's constant).

- 1 mole of carbon-12 atoms has a mass of exactly 12 g
- Molar mass (g/mol) = mass of one mole numerically equal to Ar or Mr

## Key Formulas

**n = m / Mr**
- n = number of moles
- m = mass in grams
- Mr = relative formula mass

**n = c × V (dm³)**
- n = moles
- c = concentration in mol/dm³
- V = volume in dm³

**n = V (dm³) / 24**
- For gases at r.t.p. (room temperature and pressure)
- 1 mole of any gas occupies 24 dm³ at r.t.p.

## Key Facts
- Ar: average mass of one atom relative to C-12
- Mr: sum of Ar of all atoms in one formula unit
- 1 mole = 6.02 × 10²³ (Avogadro's number)
- Moles = mass / Mr
- Moles = concentration × volume

## Making Water Potable (Safe to Drink)

### From Fresh Water (Lakes/Rivers/Reservoirs)

1. **Screening** — removes large debris (leaves, twigs, rubbish)
2. **Sedimentation** — water sits in large tanks; heavy particles settle as sludge
3. **Filtration** — water passes through sand and gravel filters to remove finer solids
4. **Chlorination** — chlorine gas (Cl₂) added to kill bacteria and microorganisms
5. (Optional) **Fluoridation** — fluoride added for dental health

### Desalination (from Seawater)

**Distillation:**
- Seawater boiled; pure water evaporates
- Water vapour condensed and collected
- Salt and impurities remain behind
- **Disadvantage**: Very energy-intensive/expensive

**Reverse Osmosis:**
- Seawater forced through a semi-permeable membrane at high pressure
- Water molecules pass through; salt ions are rejected
- More energy-efficient than distillation

## Testing Water Purity

- **Pure water** boils at exactly **100°C** at standard pressure
- **Pure water** freezes at exactly **0°C** at standard pressure
- Impure water boils OVER a range and at a higher temperature
- Impure water freezes at a LOWER temperature
- **Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate**: white → blue in presence of water (test for water)
- **Anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride**: blue → pink in presence of water

## Key Facts
- Potable = safe to drink (may contain dissolved minerals)
- Pure = only H₂O molecules (no dissolved substances)
- Chlorination kills bacteria but does NOT remove dissolved solids
- Distillation produces pure water but is expensive
- Pure water boils at exactly 100°C (at 1 atm)

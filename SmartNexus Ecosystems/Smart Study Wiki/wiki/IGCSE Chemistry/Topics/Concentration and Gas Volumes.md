# Concentration and Gas Volumes

**Summary**: Concentration in mol/dm³ or g/dm³. Molar gas volume = 24 dm³ at r.t.p. Titration uses these relationships to find unknown concentrations.

## Concentration

### Units
- **mol/dm³** (molar concentration)
- **g/dm³** (mass concentration)

### Key Formulas
- n = c × V (dm³) → moles = concentration × volume
- c (g/dm³) = c (mol/dm³) × Mr

## Molar Gas Volume

At **room temperature and pressure (r.t.p.)**, 1 mole of ANY gas occupies **24 dm³** (24,000 cm³).

- n = V (dm³) / 24
- This applies to ALL gases, regardless of their Mr
- At different temperature/pressure, the molar volume changes

## Titration Calculations

**Method:**
1. Calculate moles of known solution: n = c × V
2. Use mole ratio from balanced equation
3. Calculate unknown concentration: c = n / V

**Worked example**: 25.0 cm³ of NaOH neutralises 20.0 cm³ of 0.1 mol/dm³ HCl. Find [NaOH].
- HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O (ratio 1:1)
- Moles HCl = 0.1 × 0.020 = 0.002 mol
- Moles NaOH = 0.002 mol (1:1 ratio)
- [NaOH] = 0.002 / 0.025 = **0.08 mol/dm³**

## Unit Conversions
- 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³
- 1 m³ = 1000 dm³
- cm³ → dm³: divide by 1000

## Key Facts
- n = cV (volume in dm³)
- 1 mole of gas = 24 dm³ at r.t.p.
- In titrations, use concordant results (within 0.1 cm³)
- Single indicator — use the one that gives the sharpest colour change

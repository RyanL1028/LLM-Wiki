# Oxidation

**Summary**: Loss of electrons. Also: gain of oxygen, increase in oxidation number. OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss.
**Tags**: #igcse #chemistry #definition #reactions
**Created**: 2026-07-14
**Last Updated**: 2026-07-14

---

**Oxidation** is defined at IGCSE level in three complementary ways: as the loss of electrons (recalled by the mnemonic OIL RIG — Oxidation Is Loss), as the gain of oxygen, and as an increase in oxidation number. Oxidation never occurs in isolation; it is always coupled with reduction in a simultaneous process called a redox reaction, where one species loses electrons (is oxidised) while another gains them (is reduced). The substance that is oxidised is termed the **reducing agent** because it donates electrons and brings about reduction in the other species — a conceptual distinction that is frequently tested in exams. Common IGCSE examples include the rusting of iron (Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻, with iron being oxidised), the displacement of copper from copper(II) sulfate by zinc (Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu), and the combustion of fuels where carbon and hydrogen are oxidised by atmospheric oxygen. When writing half-equations in electrochemistry, identifying which electrode is the anode is straightforward: oxidation always takes place at the anode, whether in an electrolytic cell or a simple displacement reaction.

# Displacement Reaction

**Summary**: A reaction where a more reactive element replaces a less reactive element in a compound.
**Tags**: #igcse #chemistry #definition #reactions #metals
**Created**: 2026-07-14
**Last Updated**: 2026-07-14

---

A **displacement reaction** is a chemical reaction in which a more reactive element replaces a less reactive element from its compound, following the reactivity series. In a typical metal displacement, a more reactive metal such as zinc will displace a less reactive metal ion like copper(II) from its salt solution: Zn(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + Cu(s), with the blue colour of the solution fading as copper metal deposits on the zinc. This principle also applies to the halogens, where a more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive halide ion — for example, chlorine gas displaces bromide ions from potassium bromide solution, turning the solution brown as bromine is formed. Displacement reactions provide a practical method for determining the relative order of reactivity and are the chemical basis for sacrificial protection in corrosion prevention, where a more reactive metal such as zinc is deliberately oxidised in place of iron. IGCSE exam questions frequently ask students to predict whether a reaction will occur by consulting the reactivity series and to write balanced symbol equations with state symbols for the displacement.

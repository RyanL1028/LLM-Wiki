# Fuel Cell

**Summary**: A fuel cell is an electrochemical device that converts the chemical energy of a fuel (usually H₂) directly into electrical energy. Unlike batteries, fuel cells do not run down as long as fuel and oxygen are supplied.

## Definition

A **fuel cell** converts chemical energy directly into electrical energy via redox reactions at two electrodes, with fuel (H₂) and oxidant (O₂) supplied continuously from external sources. The only waste product from a hydrogen fuel cell is water.

## Hydrogen-Oxygen Fuel Cell

**Overall reaction**: 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l)

- **Anode (−)**: H₂ + 2OH⁻ → 2H₂O + 2e⁻ (oxidation)
- **Cathode (+)**: O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻ (reduction)
- Contains a **platinum catalyst** on porous electrodes
- **Electrolyte**: KOH(aq) or proton-exchange membrane

## Key Advantage Over Batteries

Fuel cells **do not run down** — they produce electricity continuously as long as fuel (H₂) and oxygen (O₂ from air) are supplied. Batteries have a finite amount of stored chemicals and eventually go flat.

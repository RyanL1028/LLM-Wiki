# Dehydration

**Summary**: Dehydration is the removal of water of crystallisation from a hydrated salt by heating. The hydrated salt changes colour and forms the anhydrous salt. Example: CuSO₄·5H₂O (blue) → CuSO₄ (white) + 5H₂O.

## Definition

**Dehydration** (in IGCSE Chemistry context) refers to the removal of **water of crystallisation** from a hydrated salt, usually by heating. The hydrated salt loses its water molecules and becomes anhydrous, often with a visible colour change.

## Examples

| Hydrated Salt | → | Anhydrous Salt | Colour Change |
|--------------|---|---------------|---------------|
| CuSO₄·5H₂O | → | CuSO₄ + 5H₂O | Blue → White |
| CoCl₂·6H₂O | → | CoCl₂ + 6H₂O | Pink → Blue |
| Na₂CO₃·10H₂O | → | Na₂CO₃ + 10H₂O | Colourless → White |
| FeSO₄·7H₂O | → | FeSO₄ + 7H₂O | Green → Yellow-white |

## Reversibility

Dehydration of some salts is **reversible** — adding water restores the hydrated form and original colour. This is the basis for using anhydrous CuSO₄ (white → blue) and CoCl₂ paper (blue → pink) as **tests for water**.

## Practical Note

"Heat to constant mass" — heat, cool, weigh, repeat until two consecutive weighings are equal — ensures ALL water of crystallisation has been driven off.

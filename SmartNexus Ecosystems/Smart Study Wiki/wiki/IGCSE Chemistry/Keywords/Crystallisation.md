# Crystallisation

**Summary**: Crystallisation is a separation technique used to obtain a pure soluble solid from its solution by evaporating some solvent and then allowing the solution to cool. Slow cooling produces larger, purer crystals.

## Definition

**Crystallisation** is a physical separation technique that produces solid crystals from a solution. As the solution cools, solubility decreases and the dissolved solid comes out of solution as regularly-structured crystals.

## Method

1. Heat the solution to evaporate some solvent (NOT to dryness)
2. Stop heating at the **crystallisation point** — when crystals first appear on cooling / a glass rod dipped in gives crystals
3. Allow to **cool slowly** at room temperature
4. Filter the crystals from the remaining solution
5. **Wash** with a little cold distilled water (removes soluble impurities; cold water minimises re-dissolving)
6. **Dry** between filter paper or in a warm oven

## Key Exam Points

- **Slow cooling** = larger, purer crystals (atoms have time to arrange in the crystal lattice)
- **Fast cooling** = smaller, less pure crystals (may trap impurities)
- **Cold distilled water** for washing (not hot — would dissolve the product)
- **Do NOT evaporate to dryness** — some salts decompose on strong heating; you get a powder, not crystals

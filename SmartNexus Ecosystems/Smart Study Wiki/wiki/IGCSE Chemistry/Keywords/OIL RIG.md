# OIL RIG

**Summary**: OIL RIG is the mnemonic for redox reactions: **O**xidation **I**s **L**oss of electrons; **R**eduction **I**s **G**ain of electrons. It tells you which side electrons appear on in half equations.

---

**OIL RIG** is the essential IGCSE mnemonic for remembering the electron-transfer definitions of the two halves of a redox reaction: **O**xidation **I**s **L**oss (of electrons), and **R**eduction **I**s **G**ain (of electrons). This mnemonic directly informs the construction of half-equations — when a species is oxidised, electrons appear on the product side (e.g., Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻), and when a species is reduced, electrons appear on the reactant side (e.g., Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu). An equally popular alternative taught in many classrooms is **LEO says GER** (Loss of Electrons is Oxidation; Gain of Electrons is Reduction), which conveys the same logic in a different form. OIL RIG also helps identify the reducing and oxidising agents in a reaction: the substance that loses electrons (is oxidised) acts as the reducing agent, while the substance that gains electrons (is reduced) acts as the oxidising agent — a distinction that IGCSE candidates frequently confuse. In electrochemistry, OIL RIG maps onto electrode processes: oxidation always occurs at the anode and reduction at the cathode, so remembering which process involves electron loss pinpoints the anode even in unfamiliar cell diagrams.

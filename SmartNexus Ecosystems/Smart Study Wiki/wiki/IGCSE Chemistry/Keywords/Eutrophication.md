# Eutrophication

**Summary**: Eutrophication is the process where excess nutrients (nitrates and phosphates from fertilisers) enter water bodies, causing algal blooms that deplete dissolved oxygen and kill aquatic life.

## Definition

**Eutrophication** is the enrichment of water bodies with nutrients (mainly nitrates and phosphates from agricultural fertilisers), leading to excessive plant and algal growth that ultimately depletes dissolved oxygen and kills aquatic organisms.

## Process (Step by Step)

1. **Fertiliser runoff**: Rain washes nitrate (NO₃⁻) and phosphate (PO₄³⁻) fertilisers from farmland into rivers, lakes, and streams
2. **Algal bloom**: Excess nutrients cause algae and water plants to grow rapidly — a thick green layer forms on the water surface
3. **Light blockage**: The algal layer blocks sunlight from reaching underwater plants
4. **Plant death**: Underwater plants die (cannot photosynthesise without light)
5. **Bacterial decomposition**: Aerobic bacteria decompose the dead organic matter, consuming dissolved oxygen (O₂) in the water
6. **Oxygen depletion**: Dissolved O₂ levels drop dramatically
7. **Aquatic death**: Fish and other aquatic organisms die from hypoxia (oxygen starvation)

## Sources of Eutrophying Nutrients

| Source | Nutrients Released |
|--------|--------------------|
| Agricultural fertilisers | Nitrates (NO₃⁻), phosphates (PO₄³⁻) |
| Untreated sewage | Organic waste → nitrates/phosphates |
| Animal waste (slurry/manure) | Nitrates, phosphates |
| Industrial detergents | Phosphates |

## Prevention

- Use fertiliser sparingly and at appropriate times (avoid application before heavy rain)
- Maintain buffer zones of vegetation between farmland and water bodies
- Treat sewage before discharge into rivers
- Use phosphate-free detergents

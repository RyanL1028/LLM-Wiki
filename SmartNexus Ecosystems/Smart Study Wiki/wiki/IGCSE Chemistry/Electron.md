# Electron

**Summary**: An electron is a negatively charged subatomic particle with negligible mass (relative mass 1/1836) that occupies shells around the nucleus, governs chemical bonding, and is responsible for electrical conduction in metals.
**Tags**: #igcse #chemistry
**Created**: 2026-07-18

---

The [[Electron]] is a fundamental subatomic particle that carries a relative negative charge of -1 and has an extremely small mass -- approximately 1/1836 of the mass of a [[Proton]], which is effectively negligible when calculating [[Relative Masses and Moles|relative atomic mass]]. Unlike [[Proton|protons]] and [[Neutron|neutrons]], which reside in the dense central [[Nucleus (Chemistry)|nucleus]], [[Electron|electrons]] exist outside the nucleus in specific regions called [[Electron Shell|electron shells]] (also called energy levels), where they move rapidly and occupy most of the atom's volume. The number of [[Electron|electrons]] in a neutral [[Atom (Chemistry)|atom]] equals the number of [[Proton|protons]] (the atomic number), ensuring overall electrical neutrality. [[Electron|Electrons]] are the subatomic particles most directly involved in chemical bonding: they are transferred in [[Ions and Ionic Bonds|ionic bonding]], shared in [[Covalent Bonding|covalent bonding]], and delocalised in [[Metallic Bonding|metallic bonding]]. Their arrangement, described by the [[Electronic Configuration]], determines an [[Element]]'s chemical properties, reactivity, and position in the [[Periodic Table Trends|Periodic Table]].

## Properties of the Electron

### Charge and Mass

The [[Electron]] has the following key properties:

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Relative charge | -1 |
| Actual charge | -1.602 x 10^-19 C |
| Relative mass | 1/1836 (approximately 0.0005) |
| Actual mass | 9.109 x 10^-31 kg |
| Location | [[Electron Shell|Electron shells]] surrounding the [[Nucleus (Chemistry)|nucleus]] |
| Symbol | e or e^- |

The [[Electron]] is approximately 1836 times lighter than a [[Proton]] or [[Neutron]]. Because of this negligible mass, [[Electron|electrons]] contribute virtually nothing to the total mass of an [[Atom (Chemistry)|atom]], which is essentially determined by the number of [[Proton|protons]] and [[Neutron|neutrons]] in the [[Nucleus (Chemistry)|nucleus]]. However, despite their tiny mass, [[Electron|electrons]] dominate the volume of the atom and are responsible for all ordinary chemical behaviour.

### Arrangement in Shells

[[Electron|Electrons]] are arranged around the [[Nucleus (Chemistry)|nucleus]] in specific [[Electron Shell|electron shells]] (energy levels). For the first 20 [[Element|elements]] of the [[Periodic Table Trends|Periodic Table]], the filling rules are:
- **1st shell**: holds a maximum of **2** [[Electron|electrons]]
- **2nd shell**: holds a maximum of **8** [[Electron|electrons]]
- **3rd shell**: holds a maximum of **8** [[Electron|electrons]] (for the first 20 elements)

The arrangement of [[Electron|electrons]] in these shells is called the [[Electronic Configuration]]. For example, sodium (Z = 11) has the electronic configuration 2.8.1, meaning 2 electrons in the first shell, 8 in the second, and 1 in the third (outer) shell. The number of [[Electron|electrons]] in the outermost shell determines the chemical properties and reactivity of the element. Electrons in the outermost shell are called [[Valence Electron|valence electrons]].

### Role in Chemical Bonding

[[Electron|Electrons]] are central to all three types of chemical bonding:
- **[[Ions and Ionic Bonds|Ionic bonding]]**: [[Electron|electrons]] are transferred from a metal atom to a non-metal atom. The metal loses [[Electron|electrons]] to become a positive [[Ion]] (cation), and the non-metal gains [[Electron|electrons]] to become a negative [[Ion]] (anion). The oppositely charged ions attract each other by electrostatic forces.
- **[[Covalent Bonding|Covalent bonding]]**: [[Electron|electrons]] are shared between non-metal atoms. Each shared pair of [[Electron|electrons]] forms one covalent bond, enabling each atom to achieve a full outer shell (noble gas configuration).
- **[[Metallic Bonding|Metallic bonding]]**: [[Electron|electrons]] in the outer shells of metal atoms become [[Delocalised Electron|delocalised]] -- free to move throughout the [[Giant Lattice|metal lattice]]. This sea of delocalised electrons holds the positive metal ions together.

In all cases, atoms bond in order to achieve a stable outer-shell electron arrangement, typically a full outer shell of 2 or 8 [[Electron|electrons]] (the noble gas configuration). This is sometimes called the octet rule.

### Role in Electrical Conduction

[[Electron|Electrons]] are the charge carriers responsible for electrical conductivity:
- In [[Metallic Bonding|metals]] and [[Giant Structures|graphite]], [[Delocalised Electron|delocalised electrons]] can move freely through the structure, carrying electric current.
- In [[Electrolysis|electrolysis]], [[Electron|electrons]] are transferred between species at the electrodes, enabling the flow of current through the external circuit.
- Covalent [[Compound|compounds]] (simple molecular structures) do not conduct electricity because there are no mobile charged particles -- all [[Electron|electrons]] are either held in covalent bonds or as lone pairs within the [[Molecule|molecules]], and the [[Molecule|molecules]] themselves are neutral.

The gain or loss of [[Electron|electrons]] also defines [[Redox Reactions|oxidation and reduction]]: oxidation is the loss of [[Electron|electrons]], and reduction is the gain of [[Electron|electrons]] (OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain).

## Common Misconceptions

| Misconception | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Electrons orbit the nucleus in fixed circular paths like planets" | Electrons occupy shells (energy levels). The shell model describes energy levels and probabilities, not fixed orbits like planets around the Sun |
| "Electrons are stationary within their shells" | Electrons are in constant rapid motion around the nucleus. The shells represent energy levels, not fixed positions |
| "The electron mass contributes significantly to atomic mass" | The mass of an electron is approximately 1/1836 of a proton -- negligible for atomic mass calculations. Atomic mass is essentially the sum of proton and neutron masses |
| "Electrons and protons attract each other by gravity" | The attraction between electrons and the nucleus is electrostatic (electromagnetic), not gravitational. Gravity is negligible at the atomic scale |
| "When atoms lose electrons, protons are also lost" | Only electrons are lost or gained in chemical reactions and ion formation. The number of protons in the nucleus never changes during chemical processes |

# Proton

**Summary**: A proton is a positively charged subatomic particle found in the nucleus of an atom, with relative charge +1 and relative mass 1, whose number defines the atomic number and identity of the element.
**Tags**: #igcse #chemistry
**Created**: 2026-07-18

---

The [[Proton]] is one of the three fundamental subatomic particles that make up an [[Atom (Chemistry)|atom]], alongside the [[Neutron]] and the [[Electron]]. It resides in the [[Nucleus (Chemistry)|nucleus]] and carries a relative positive charge of +1 (equal and opposite in magnitude to the charge of an [[Electron]]), with a relative mass of 1 on the atomic mass scale. The number of [[Proton|protons]] in the nucleus is called the atomic number (Z), and this number is the sole determinant of which [[Element]] an atom belongs to -- changing the number of [[Proton|protons]] transforms the atom into a different [[Element]] entirely. Because [[Proton|protons]] are positively charged, they experience electrostatic repulsion from one another within the [[Nucleus (Chemistry)|nucleus]], but they are held together by the strong nuclear force, which also acts on [[Neutron|neutrons]]. The identity and chemical behaviour of every [[Element]] in the [[Periodic Table Trends|Periodic Table]] is ultimately determined by its number of [[Proton|protons]].

## Properties of the Proton

### Charge and Mass

The [[Proton]] has the following key properties:

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Relative charge | +1 |
| Actual charge | +1.602 x 10^-19 C (elementary charge) |
| Relative mass | 1 |
| Actual mass | 1.673 x 10^-27 kg |
| Location | [[Nucleus (Chemistry)|Nucleus]] |
| Symbol | p or p^+ |

The [[Proton]] is approximately 1836 times heavier than an [[Electron]]. In neutral atoms, the number of [[Proton|protons]] in the [[Nucleus (Chemistry)|nucleus]] equals the number of [[Electron|electrons]] in the shells, giving the atom an overall charge of zero.

### Atomic Number and Element Identity

The defining characteristic of any [[Element]] is its atomic number (Z), which is the number of [[Proton|protons]] in the nucleus of each atom of that element. All atoms of a given [[Element]] have the same number of [[Proton|protons]]. For example:
- Hydrogen (H): Z = 1, one [[Proton]]
- Carbon (C): Z = 6, six [[Proton|protons]]
- Oxygen (O): Z = 8, eight [[Proton|protons]]
- Iron (Fe): Z = 26, twenty-six [[Proton|protons]]

If the number of [[Proton|protons]] in an atom changes, the atom becomes a different [[Element]] -- this is the principle behind nuclear transmutation. The number of [[Proton|protons]] in an atom never changes during ordinary chemical reactions; it only changes in nuclear reactions.

### Role in the Nucleus

Within the [[Nucleus (Chemistry)|nucleus]], [[Proton|protons]] contribute both mass and positive charge. The mass number (A) of an atom is the sum of [[Proton|protons]] and [[Neutron|neutrons]] (A = Z + N). The positive charge of the [[Proton|protons]] in the [[Nucleus (Chemistry)|nucleus]] creates the electrostatic attraction that holds the negatively charged [[Electron|electrons]] in their [[Electron Shell|electron shells]] around the nucleus. Without this electrostatic force between nucleus and electrons, atoms could not exist as stable entities.

The [[Proton|protons]] in the nucleus also participate in the formation of [[Ion|ions]]. When an atom loses or gains [[Electron|electrons]] to form an [[Ion]], the number of [[Proton|protons]] in the nucleus remains unchanged. For instance, a sodium atom (11 [[Proton|protons]], 11 [[Electron|electrons]]) becomes a Na+ ion (still 11 [[Proton|protons]], but only 10 [[Electron|electrons]]).

### Proton Number and the Periodic Table

The [[Periodic Table Trends|Periodic Table]] arranges [[Element|elements]] in order of increasing atomic number (proton number). This ordering reflects a fundamental principle: the properties of [[Element|elements]] are periodic functions of their atomic numbers. The position of an [[Element]] in the [[Periodic Table Trends|Periodic Table]] -- its period and group -- is determined by the electron configuration, which in a neutral atom is dictated by the number of [[Proton|protons]].

## Common Misconceptions

| Misconception | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Protons and electrons have the same mass" | Protons are approximately 1836 times heavier than electrons. Nearly all the mass of an atom comes from protons and neutrons |
| "The number of protons can change in chemical reactions" | The number of protons never changes in chemical reactions -- only electrons are transferred or shared. Protons change only in nuclear reactions |
| "All atoms of the same element have the same number of protons and neutrons" | All atoms of an element have the same number of protons (same Z), but the number of neutrons can differ (different [[Isotope|isotopes]]) |
| "A positive ion has gained protons" | A positive ion (cation) has lost electrons, not gained protons. The proton number stays the same |

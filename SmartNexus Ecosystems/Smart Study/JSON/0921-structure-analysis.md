# 0921 IGCSE Chemistry — Past Paper Structure Analysis
- by Ryan and ClaudeCode
---

## 1. Overview

This analysis covers *38 IGCSE Chemistry past papers* (0620 and 0971) for which both the question paper and mark scheme were available in extractable (text-based) PDF format. The extraction is *MS-driven* — mark scheme data provides the structural backbone, while question paper text supplies context.

---

## 2. Syllabus Codes

The dataset spans two Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry syllabi:

| Code | Papers | Description |
|------|--------|-------------|
| **0620/33** | 17 | IGCSE Chemistry — Paper 3 Theory (Core) |
| **0620/32** | 10 | IGCSE Chemistry — Paper 3 Theory (Core, variant 2) |
| **0971/32** | 8 | IGCSE Chemistry (9–1) — Paper 3 Theory (Core) |
| **0971/31** | 2 | IGCSE Chemistry (9–1) — Paper 3 Theory (Core, variant 1) |
| **0620/31** | 1 | IGCSE Chemistry — Paper 3 Theory (Core, variant 1) |

**Key distinction**: 0620 is the legacy A\*–G grading syllabus; 0971 is the newer 9–1 grading syllabus. Their paper structures and content are nearly identical — the 9–1 syllabus has slightly more modern question phrasing and updated topic emphasis.

## 3. Paper Structure

### 3.1 Top-Level Format

Every paper follows the same blueprint:

- **Duration**: 1 hour 15 minutes (75 minutes)
- **Total marks**: 80
- **Questions**: 4–8 per paper (average: 6)
- **Parts per question**: 1–12 (average: ~5)
- **Answer format**: Write on the question paper (no separate answer booklet)
- **Provided**: Periodic Table printed in the paper

### 3.2 Parts per Question

Questions are broken into labeled sub-parts (`(a)`, `(a)(i)`, `(b)`, etc.):

| Parts per question | Frequency | % |
|-------------------|-----------|----|
| 2–3 parts | 72 questions | 29% |
| 4–5 parts | 82 questions | 33% |
| 6–7 parts | 69 questions | 28% |
| 8+ parts | 27 questions | 11% |

Most questions have **4–7 sub-parts**, each worth 1–2 marks.

### 3.3 Marks per Question

| Marks per question | Frequency | % |
|-------------------|-----------|----|
| 2–3 marks | 54 | 22% |
| 4–5 marks | 46 | 19% |
| 6–7 marks | 68 | 28% |
| 8–9 marks | 40 | 16% |
| 10+ marks | 27 | 11% |

Most questions carry **4–9 marks** with a strong center at **6–7 marks** per question.

### 3.4 Mark Value Distribution

| Mark value | Count | % |
|-----------|-------|----|
| 1 mark | 834 | 68% |
| 2 marks | 251 | 20% |
| 3 marks | 20 | 1.6% |
| 4 marks | 14 | 1.1% |
| 0 (descriptive) | 106 | 9% |

**~68% of all parts are 1-mark questions** — quick recall of facts, names, symbols, or single-step answers.  
**~20% are 2-mark questions** — explanations, descriptions, or calculations with working.  
**3–4 mark questions** are rare (~3%) and typically extended writing or multi-step calculations.

### 3.5 Question Types

| Type | Count | % | Typical use |
|------|-------|----|-------------|
| **Short answer** | 1,007 | 82% | State, name, identify, give |
| **Long answer** | 178 | 14% | Describe, explain, suggest, discuss |
| **Multiple choice** | 30 | 2% | Tick-box, matching |
| **Diagram** | 10 | <1% | Label apparatus, draw structure |

The paper is overwhelmingly **short-answer** (82%). Extended writing and diagram tasks are uncommon but appear in nearly every paper (typically 1–2 long questions and 1 diagram per paper).

### 3.6 Part Labeling Convention

All parts follow the Cambridge standard:

- `1(a)(i)`, `1(a)(ii)`, `1(a)(iii)` — sub-parts of part (a)
- `1(b)` — next major part
- `2(a)`, `2(b)(i)`, `2(b)(ii)` — same pattern for question 2

Format: **`question(letter)(roman)`** — e.g., `3(c)(ii)`

## 4. Topic Coverage

### 4.1 Topic Distribution

Topics are identified from mark scheme answer text matching against curriculum keywords:

| Topic | Questions | Avg parts/Q | Avg marks/Q | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| **Metals & Reactivity** | 28 | 5.1 | 6.0 | Reactivity series, extraction, alloys, corrosion |
| **Acids, Bases & Salts** | 23 | 6.1 | 7.3 | pH, indicators, neutralisation, salt preparation |
| **Organic Chemistry** | 22 | 6.0 | 7.0 | Alkanes/alkenes, polymers, alcohols, esters |
| **Electrolysis** | 13 | 4.9 | 7.3 | Molten electrolysis, aqueous, electroplating |
| **Sulfur & Compounds** | 9 | 5.7 | 5.9 | Contact process, Haber process, fertilisers |
| **Atomic Structure & Periodic Table** | 8 | 5.2 | 6.2 | Electron config, isotopes, group trends |
| **Air, Water & Environment** | 5 | 7.0 | 9.0 | Pollutants, greenhouse, water treatment |
| **Stoichiometry & Moles** | 5 | 5.0 | 5.6 | Formula mass, moles, concentration, balancing |
| **Bonding & Structure** | 3 | 6.3 | 5.7 | Ionic/covalent/metallic, dot-and-cross |
| **Mixed Chemistry** | 136 | 4.2 | 5.0 | Multi-topic or unclassified questions |

**Note**: "Mixed Chemistry" dominates because many questions span multiple topics (e.g., a question about extracting iron covers Metals, Redox, and Environment simultaneously) and mark scheme answers alone are too brief for precise single-topic classification.

### 4.2 Topic Stability

These topics appear in **nearly every paper**:

- **Metals & Reactivity** — present in ~74% of papers
- **Organic Chemistry** — present in ~58%
- **Acids, Bases & Salts** — present in ~61%
- **Electrolysis** — present in ~34%

Topics that rotate in/out include Energy Changes, Chemical Analysis, and specific industrial processes (Sulfur, Haber).

## 5. Paper Evolution (2015–2024)

### 5.1 Structure Stability

The paper structure has been **remarkably stable** across the decade:

| Period | Papers | Avg questions | Avg parts | Notes |
|--------|--------|---------------|-----------|-------|
| 2015–2017 | 8 | 6.3 | 29.8 | 0620 only |
| 2018–2020 | 9 | 6.6 | 32.1 | 0971 introduced alongside 0620 |
| 2021–2024 | 21 | 6.4 | 33.2 | Both syllabi active, slightly more parts |

The shift from ~30 parts to ~33 parts per paper reflects a slight increase in sub-division — more small 1-mark parts rather than larger multi-mark blocks.

### 5.2 Syllabus Differences

| Feature | 0620 (A\*–G) | 0971 (9–1) |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| Papers in dataset | 28 | 10 |
| Avg parts/paper | 31.8 | 33.0 |
| Avg questions/paper | 6.5 | 6.6 |
| Topic emphasis | Balanced | Slightly more Organic & Environment |
| Question style | Traditional | More context-based scenarios |

The differences are minor — the syllabi test the same chemistry at the same level.

### 6.2 Limitations

- **25 legacy papers** (pre-2015 LJ/LN/S series) have image-based mark schemes with no extractable text
- **Q1 missing in 15 papers**: Q1 is often a diagram/labeling question where the MS uses a different format; the MS has no discrete Q1 entries for these papers
- **106 zero-mark parts** (9%): MS entries that are descriptions or rubric rather than scorable answers
- **Topic classification**: 136/252 questions (54%) are "Mixed Chemistry" — topic detection from MS answers alone is inherently limited without QP question text

---

## 7. Key Takeaways for Mock Paper Design

1. **80 marks, 6–8 questions, 75 minutes** — this is the fixed template
2. **~32 parts per paper, ~68% are 1-mark** — design with lots of quick-recall items
3. **Every paper has**: at least one calculation question, one diagram/labeling task, and 1–2 extended writing parts
4. **Core guaranteed topics**: Metals, Organic, Acids/Bases appear in most papers
5. **Question format**: short answer dominates (82%), with 2–3 longer explanation parts per paper
6. **Part labeling**: Always use `(a)(i)`, `(a)(ii)`, `(b)` style — never deviate

## 1. ATOMIC STRUCTURE AND SYMBOLISM
**Source:** OpenStax Chemistry 2e, Section 2.3

### Subatomic Particles

| Particle | Location | Charge | Mass |
|----------|----------|--------|------|
| Proton | Nucleus | 1+ (1.602 x 10^-19 C) | 1.0073 amu |
| Neutron | Nucleus | 0 (neutral) | 1.0087 amu |
| Electron | Outside nucleus | 1- (-1.602 x 10^-19 C) | 0.00055 amu (~1/1800 of proton) |

- The nucleus is about 100,000 times smaller than the atom itself.
- The nucleus contains the majority of the atom's mass; electrons occupy almost all of its volume.

### Atomic Number (Z)
The number of protons in the nucleus. Defines the element's identity. In a neutral atom, protons = electrons.

### Mass Number (A)
Total number of protons + neutrons in the nucleus.
```
Number of neutrons = A - Z
```

### Isotopes
Atoms of the same element (same Z) with different mass numbers (different neutron counts). Chemically identical.
- Notation: mass number as left superscript (e.g., ^24Mg, ^12C)
- Hydrogen isotopes: ^1H (protium), ^2H (deuterium/D), ^3H (tritium/T)

### Ions
- **Cation:** Positively charged (lost electrons)
- **Anion:** Negatively charged (gained electrons)
- Atomic charge = number of protons - number of electrons

### Average Atomic Mass
Weighted average of all naturally occurring isotopes:
```
average mass = sum(fractional abundance x isotopic mass)
```

## 2. THE PERIODIC TABLE
**Source:** OpenStax Chemistry 2e, Section 2.5

### Periodic Law
Properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers.

### Organization
- 7 horizontal **periods** (rows)
- 18 vertical **groups** (columns, IUPAC numbering 1-18)

### Element Classification

| Category | Location | Properties |
|----------|----------|------------|
| Metals | Left/center (yellow) | Shiny, malleable, ductile, good conductors |
| Nonmetals | Upper right (green) | Dull, poor conductors |
| Metalloids | Diagonal boundary (purple) | Intermediate properties |
| Main-group elements | Groups 1, 2, 13-18 | s and p block |
| Transition metals | Groups 3-12 | d block |
| Inner transition metals | Two bottom rows | Lanthanides + Actinides |

### Named Groups

|-------|------|
| 1 | Alkali metals |
| 2 | Alkaline earth metals |
| 15 | Pnictogens |
| 16 | Chalcogens |
| 17 | Halogens |

## 3. IONIC BONDING
**Source:** OpenStax Chemistry 2e, Section 7.1

### Key Definitions
- **Ionic bond:** Electrostatic force of attraction between oppositely charged cations and anions.
- **Ionic compound (salt):** Compound composed of ions held together by ionic bonds.
- **Lattice energy:** Energy required to separate one mole of an ionic solid into its gaseous ions. A measure of bond strength.

### Ion Formation Rules
- **Metals** (low ionization energy) -> lose electrons -> **cations**
- **Nonmetals** (high electron affinity) -> gain electrons -> **anions**

**Cation charge:**
- Groups 1 & 2: charge = group number (Na+, Ca2+)
- Groups 13-17: charge = group number - 10 (Al3+)
- Transition metals: lose s electrons first, then d electrons

**Anion charge:**
- Charge = group number - 18 (or group number - 8) (e.g., O2-, Cl-)

### Ionic Formulas
- Simplest whole-number ratio for electrical neutrality
- Total positive charge = total negative charge
- Ionic compounds form 3D lattice structures, not discrete molecules
- Smallest repeating unit = **formula unit**

### Lattice Energy Trends
- Lattice energy increases with higher ionic charges and smaller ionic radii
- Coulomb's Law: deltaH_lattice = C(Z+)(Z-)/Ro

| Compound | Lattice Energy (kJ/mol) |
|----------|------------------------|
| LiF | 1024 |
| NaCl | 788 |
| MgF2 | 2910 |
| MgO | 3938 |

### Properties of Ionic Compounds
- Crystalline, rigid, but brittle
- High melting and boiling points
- Poor conductors when solid; excellent conductors when molten or dissolved
- Generally dissolve readily in water

## 4. COVALENT BONDING
**Source:** OpenStax Chemistry 2e, Section 7.2

### Key Definitions
- **Covalent bond:** Electrons shared between atoms, attracted by both nuclei.
- **Bond length (bond distance):** Distance between nuclei at minimum potential energy.
- **Bond energy (bond dissociation energy):** Energy required to break a covalent bond in a gaseous substance (kJ/mol).
- **Electronegativity:** Tendency of an atom to attract electrons in a bond (Pauling scale, 0-4).

### Electronegativity and Bond Type
| deltaEN Range | Bond Type |
|---------------|-----------|
| 0 to <0.4 | Pure (nonpolar) covalent |
| 0.4 to ~1.8 | Polar covalent |
| >1.8 | Ionic |

- **Fluorine** is the most electronegative element (EN = 4.0)
- Electronegativity increases left to right, decreases down a group

### Pure vs. Polar Covalent Bonds
- **Nonpolar:** Electrons shared equally (e.g., H2, Cl2)
- **Polar:** Unequal sharing; more electronegative atom gets partial negative (delta-), less electronegative gets partial positive (delta+)

### Lewis Structures
- Lewis symbols: dots represent valence electrons
- Lewis structures: show lone pairs and bonding pairs
- **Octet rule:** Most atoms (except H) surrounded by 8 valence electrons
- Single bond = 1 shared pair; double bond = 2 shared pairs; triple bond = 3 shared pairs

### Bond Energy Reference
- H-H bond dissociation energy: 436 kJ/mol
- Breaking bonds = endothermic; forming bonds = exothermic

## 5. STOICHIOMETRY
**Source:** OpenStax Chemistry 2e, Section 4.3

### Key Constants and Definitions
- **Avogadro's number (NA):** 6.022 x 10^23 particles/mol
- **Mole:** Amount of substance containing Avogadro's number of particles
- **Molar mass:** Mass in grams of one mole (g/mol); equals atomic/formula mass from periodic table

### Stoichiometric Calculations Flow
```
grams A -> moles A -> moles B -> grams B
```

### Key Formulas
| Concept | Formula |
|---------|---------|
| Moles from mass | moles = mass (g) / molar mass (g/mol) |
| Particles from moles | particles = moles x 6.022 x 10^23 |
| Stoichiometric conversion | moles B = moles A x (coefficient B / coefficient A) |
| Percent yield | (actual yield / theoretical yield) x 100% |

### Limiting Reactant
The reactant completely consumed first; determines maximum product amount.

**Finding the limiting reactant:**
1. Convert all reactant masses to moles
2. Divide each by its stoichiometric coefficient
3. Smallest result = limiting reactant

### Yields
- **Theoretical yield:** Maximum product predicted by stoichiometry
- **Actual yield:** Amount actually obtained from experiment
- **Percent yield:** (actual / theoretical) x 100%

## 6. BRONSTED-LOWRY ACIDS AND BASES
**Source:** OpenStax Chemistry 2e, Section 14.1

### Definitions
- **Bronsted-Lowry acid:** Proton (H+) donor
- **Bronsted-Lowry base:** Proton (H+) acceptor
- Acid-base reaction = proton transfer from acid to base

### Conjugate Acid-Base Pairs
```
HA + H2O <-> H3O+ + A-
```
- A- is the conjugate base of HA
- H3O+ is the conjugate acid of H2O

### Amphiprotic Species
Can act as both proton donors and proton acceptors. Water is the most important example.

Bicarbonate ion (HCO3-) example:
- As acid: HCO3- + H2O <-> CO3^2- + H3O+
- As base: HCO3- + H2O <-> H2CO3 + OH-

### Autoionization of Water
```
2H2O(l) <-> H3O+(aq) + OH-(aq)
Kw = [H3O+][OH-] = 1.0 x 10^-14 at 25 C
```
In pure water at 25 C: [H3O+] = [OH-] = 1.0 x 10^-7 M

## 7. ELECTROCHEMISTRY (REDOX CHEMISTRY)
**Source:** OpenStax Chemistry 2e, Section 17.1

### Oxidation Numbers
- Elemental form: oxidation number = 0
- In compounds: charge the atom would have if purely ionic
- Sum of oxidation numbers = overall charge of species

### Key Definitions
| Term | Definition | Oxidation Number Change |
|------|------------|------------------------|
| Oxidation | Loss of electrons | Increase |
| Reduction | Gain of electrons | Decrease |
| Oxidizing agent | Substance reduced (gains electrons) | -- |
| Reducing agent | Substance oxidized (loses electrons) | -- |

### Half-Reaction Method (Balancing Redox in Acidic Solution)
1. Write skeletal oxidation and reduction half-reactions
2. Balance atoms except H and O
3. Balance O by adding H2O
4. Balance H by adding H+
5. Balance charge by adding electrons (e-)
6. Equalize electrons between half-reactions
7. Add half-reactions, cancel common species
8. Check atom and charge balance

### Additional Step for Basic Solutions
Add OH- to both sides to neutralize H+ (converting them to H2O), then simplify.

## 8. ORGANIC CHEMISTRY (HYDROCARBONS)
**Source:** OpenStax Chemistry 2e, Section 20.1

### Hydrocarbon Classification

| Type | Bonding | General Formula | Hybridization | Example |
|------|---------|-----------------|---------------|---------|
| Alkanes | Single C-C bonds | CnH2n+2 | sp3 | CH4, C2H6 |
| Alkenes | At least one C=C | -- | sp2 (at C=C) | C2H4 (ethene) |
| Alkynes | At least one C triple bond C | -- | sp (at triple bond) | C2H2 (ethyne) |

- **Alkanes:** Saturated hydrocarbons; combustion yields CO2 + H2O
- **Alkenes:** Unsaturated; more reactive due to pi bond
- **Alkynes:** Contain triple bonds

### Isomerism
- **Structural (constitutional) isomers:** Same formula, different atom connectivity
- **Geometric (cis-trans) isomers:** Possible in alkenes due to restricted rotation around double bond

### IUPAC Nomenclature for Alkanes
1. Find the longest continuous carbon chain (base name)
2. Number from the end nearest a substituent
3. Name substituents as prefixes (alphabetical), using di-, tri-, tetra- for multiples
4. Alkyl groups: -ane -> -yl (methyl, ethyl, propyl, etc.)

## 9. THERMOCHEMISTRY (ENTHALPY)
**Source:** OpenStax Chemistry 2e, Section 5.3

### Key Definitions
- **Enthalpy (H):** State function; H = U + PV
- **Enthalpy change (deltaH):** At constant pressure, deltaH = qp (heat flow)
- **Exothermic:** deltaH < 0 (negative); heat released
- **Endothermic:** deltaH > 0 (positive); heat absorbed
- Reversing a reaction flips the sign of deltaH

### Thermochemical Equations
Conventions:
1. Sign of deltaH indicates endothermic (+) or exothermic (-)
2. Coefficients = moles; deltaH is proportional (extensive property)
3. Phases must be specified (deltaH depends on physical state)
4. Values apply when reactants and products at same temperature (usually 25 C)

### Hess's Law
If a process can be written as the sum of stepwise processes, the enthalpy change of the total process equals the sum of the enthalpy changes of the individual steps (deltaH is path-independent).

### Standard Enthalpy of Formation (deltaHf)
- Enthalpy change when 1 mole of a compound forms from its elements in their standard states (1 bar, 298.15 K)
- deltaHf = 0 for any element in its standard state

### Calculating Reaction Enthalpy
```
deltaH_reaction = sum[n x deltaHf(products)] - sum[n x deltaHf(reactants)]
```

## 10. CHEMICAL BONDING AND MOLECULAR GEOMETRY
**Source:** OpenStax Chemistry 2e, Sections 7.3-7.6

### VSEPR Theory
Valence Shell Electron-Pair Repulsion: electron pairs in the valence shell adopt arrangements that minimize repulsion by maximizing distance. Each single, double, or triple bond = one region of electron density.

### Electron-Pair Geometries
| Regions | Geometry | Ideal Bond Angles |
|---------|----------|-------------------|
| 2 | Linear | 180 |
| 3 | Trigonal planar | 120 |
| 4 | Tetrahedral | 109.5 |
| 5 | Trigonal bipyramidal | 90 (axial-eq), 120 (eq-eq) |
| 6 | Octahedral | 90 |

### Molecular Shapes
| Regions | Lone Pairs | Molecular Shape | Example |
|---------|------------|-----------------|---------|
| 2 | 0 | Linear | CO2, BeF2 |
| 3 | 0 | Trigonal planar | BCl3, BF3 |
| 3 | 1 | Bent | SO2 |
| 4 | 0 | Tetrahedral | CH4, SiCl4 |
| 4 | 1 | Trigonal pyramidal | NH3, PF3 |
| 4 | 2 | Bent | H2O, OF2 |
| 5 | 0 | Trigonal bipyramidal | PF5 |
| 5 | 1 | Seesaw | SF4 |
| 5 | 2 | T-shaped | ClF3 |
| 5 | 3 | Linear | XeF2 |
| 6 | 0 | Octahedral | SF6 |
| 6 | 1 | Square pyramidal | BrF5 |
| 6 | 2 | Square planar | XeF4 |

### Repulsion Order (greatest to least)
lone pair-lone pair > lone pair-bonding pair > bonding pair-bonding pair

### Molecular Polarity
Depends on both bond polarities AND molecular shape:
- Symmetrical geometry with equal bond dipoles = **nonpolar** (CO2, BF3, CH4)
- Asymmetrical geometry or unequal dipoles = **polar** (H2O, NH3)

**Dipole moment:** mu = Q x r (charge x distance)

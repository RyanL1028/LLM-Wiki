Remember when "AI marking" was just counting how many times you wrote the word "because"? Yeah, we cringed too.

## The Old "AI" — Let's Be Honest

For months, our platform's "AI feedback" was doing the digital equivalent of a teacher who only reads the first sentence of your essay. Word counting. Keyword matching with `.includes()`. A hardcoded list of "strengths" and "gaps" that had exactly zero understanding of what you actually wrote.

It was like having a robot TA that just checked if your answer was long enough and contained the word "therefore." Technically "AI" in the same way a calculator is a "math professor."

## The New Brain: DeepSeek

We ripped out 150 lines of embarrassing rule-based code and replaced it with an actual large language model. Every answer you submit now goes to **DeepSeek's neural network** — a model that actually *reads* your answer, *understands* the chemistry (or CS, or physics) concepts, and gives feedback that's specific to what YOU wrote.

The new AI notices when you almost got it right — not just whether you hit the keywords. It tells you *why* your explanation was close but not quite there. It generates model answers tailored to the question, not generic templates. It recommends study topics based on YOUR gaps, not a predetermined list.

## Chemistry Has Entered the Chat

We dumped **68 IGCSE Chemistry past papers** into the platform. 0971, 0620, Core, Extended, International, Legacy — the entire Cambridge Chemistry catalog from 2002 to 2024. That's over 500 structured questions with mark schemes, all digitized into our exam engine with keyword-aware marking.

Combined with the new DeepSeek AI, you can now:
- Answer a Chemistry question about electrolysis
- Get feedback that understands the difference between the anode and cathode products
- Receive improvement tips that reference actual IGCSE syllabus topics
- See a model answer written by an AI that knows the difference between thermal decomposition and combustion

## The Cover Page That Should Have Existed All Along

Every exam paper now starts on a clean, standalone front cover — just like the real thing. Cambridge crest, subject, paper code, instructions, the whole deal. No timer ticking in your face. No questions peeking through. Just you, the instructions, and a "Begin Exam" button that starts the clock. Because reading the instructions shouldn't feel like you're already losing time.
